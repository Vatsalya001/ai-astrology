The twelfth house is vyaya bhava — the house of expenditure — and the last of the three
dusthanas. It is also the house classical texts associate with moksha, which makes it
the clearest example in the system of a difficult house with a valued signification.

## What it governs

Loss and expenditure, foreign residence, isolation and confinement, sleep and dreams,
charity, spiritual practice, the feet, and whatever the native gives up.

## Loss and liberation in one house

The classical reasoning for the pairing is that both are forms of letting go, and that
the difference is whether it is chosen. Expenditure on something valued is read
differently from expenditure that simply happens, and the twelfth does not distinguish
them on its own — the rest of the chart does.

Modern readings often resolve this tension by picking the pleasant meaning. The
classical sources do not: they treat the twelfth as costly, and treat the spiritual
reading as what becomes available when the cost is accepted rather than as a
substitute for it.

## How it is read

- The twelfth lords condition for where the natives resources go
- Its relationship to the sixth and eighth for health questions, since the three
  dusthanas are read together
- Planets here as significations the native experiences privately or abroad

## Foreign residence

The twelfths association with distant places is one of its most practically useful
significations, and it pairs with the ninth: the ninth is travel undertaken, the
twelfth is residence that separates the native from their origin.

## A caution

A strong twelfth is read as capacity for solitude and for giving, not as a guarantee of
either spiritual progress or of loss. Of all twelve houses this is the one where the
difference between the classical reading and the popular one is widest.
