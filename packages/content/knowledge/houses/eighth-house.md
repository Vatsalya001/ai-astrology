The eighth house is the most difficult of the dusthanas to read well, because its
classical significations include longevity and modern readings routinely overreach on
it.

## What it governs

Transformation, sudden change, inheritance and the resources of others, insurance,
occult and research disciplines, hidden things, the reproductive and excretory systems,
and longevity.

## What it does not license

The eighth is traditionally called ayur bhava — the house of lifespan — and classical
texts contain elaborate methods for estimating longevity from it. Those methods are
deliberately not developed in this corpus.

A reading must never produce a statement about death, its timing, or its manner. The
reasons are practical as well as ethical: the classical longevity methods disagree with
each other, they were designed for a context in which the question was asked
differently, and a wrong answer is unfalsifiable until it is catastrophic. There is no
version of this that is responsible to tell someone.

## How it is read, usefully

- Transformation rather than ending: the eighth is where things change form
- The resources of others — inheritance, a partners money, institutional funds — which
  is why it matters in financial readings alongside the second and eleventh
- Research and depth: disciplines that require sustained attention to what is not
  visible
- Its lords condition for how the native handles discontinuity

## A caution

A strong eighth is traditionally associated with resilience through change rather than
with the absence of it. Natives with a prominent eighth frequently report lives with
sharp discontinuities and considerable capacity to absorb them, which is the useful
version of what this house describes.
