The planetary mantras are among the most widely used traditional remedies, and among
the least problematic — they cost nothing and are a devotional practice in their own
right rather than a transaction.

## The traditional associations

Each of the nine grahas has associated mantras: a short beeja (seed) mantra, and longer
Vedic verses. The Navagraha Stotra addresses all nine. Specific deities are associated
with specific planets — Hanuman with Mars and with Saturn in some traditions, Vishnu
with Jupiter, Durga with the Moon, and so on, with regional variation.

Recitation counts are traditionally specified, often as multiples associated with the
planet's dasha years, and practice is often tied to the planet's weekday.

## Why this is the least troublesome remedy category

It involves no purchase, no urgency, and no intermediary. A native who recites a mantra
and finds nothing changes has lost nothing. A native who buys a blue sapphire and finds
nothing changes has lost a significant sum.

It is also a devotional practice with meaning independent of the astrology, which many
natives are already engaged in for reasons that have nothing to do with a chart.

## What this product says about it

Describes the tradition when asked. Names the correspondence. Does not prescribe a
count, a schedule or an outcome, and does not suggest that omitting it carries a cost.

## What it does not say

That recitation will produce a result. That it will avert something. That a particular
number of repetitions is required. All three cross from describing a practice into
promising an effect, and the second one requires a prediction this product does not
make.

## The honest framing

Mantra practice is a real part of the tradition this product draws on, and a native
interested in it is engaging with something genuine. It is not a mechanism this product
claims to understand or to be able to direct.
