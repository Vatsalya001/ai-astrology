The remedy category the classical sources themselves rate most highly, and the one
modern commercial practice mentions least — for reasons that are not hard to work out.

## What the tradition says

Dana (giving) and seva (service) are prescribed throughout the classical literature,
often with planet-specific detail: black sesame or iron for Saturn, jaggery or wheat
for the Sun, white cloth or rice for the Moon. Feeding people, feeding animals, and
service at a temple appear across sources.

Fasting on a planet's weekday is a related practice with the same character.

## The behavioural version, which is the strongest claim in the literature

Several classical sources make a point that deserves more attention than it gets: the
most effective remedy for a difficult planet is **to do what that planet signifies**.

- A difficult Saturn: take on responsibility, do unglamorous work, be patient where
  patience is not rewarded
- A difficult Sun: stop waiting to be recognised and act as though the authority is
  yours
- A difficult Mars: direct the energy at something that resists, rather than at people
- A difficult Jupiter: teach something, or learn something properly
- A difficult Venus: invest in a relationship rather than in comfort

This is the most useful remedial material in the tradition, and it has a property the
gemstone literature does not: it is actionable, costs nothing, and is good advice
whether or not the astrology is true.

## Why the commercial practice omits it

Because nothing is sold. A remedy that consists of "do the work" generates no revenue,
which is a sufficient explanation for its absence from the advertisements and its
presence in the texts.

## How to answer

Describe the traditional practices. Give the behavioural reading particular weight,
because the sources do. Note that the classical remedy for a difficult placement is
usually engagement with what it signifies rather than a purchase.

Do not prescribe. Do not attach an outcome. Do not suggest urgency.
