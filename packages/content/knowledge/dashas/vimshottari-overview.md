Vimshottari is the dominant timing system in Vedic astrology: a 120-year cycle divided
into nine planetary periods, in a fixed order and with fixed lengths.

## The sequence and the lengths

| Planet | Years |
|---|---|
| Ketu | 7 |
| Venus | 20 |
| Sun | 6 |
| Moon | 10 |
| Mars | 7 |
| Rahu | 18 |
| Jupiter | 16 |
| Saturn | 19 |
| Mercury | 17 |

They sum to **120** — the name means "one hundred and twenty" — and the order is fixed.
The cycle always runs Ketu → Venus → Sun → Moon → Mars → Rahu → Jupiter → Saturn →
Mercury and back to Ketu.

## Where the cycle starts

From the **Moon's nakshatra at birth**, not from the ascendant or the Sun. Each of the
27 nakshatras has a ruling planet, and that planet's period is the one in progress at
birth — partially elapsed, in proportion to how far the Moon has travelled through the
nakshatra.

The traditional reasoning is that the mind experiences a period, so periods are counted
from the significator of mind.

## Why birth time matters so much

The Moon crosses a nakshatra in roughly a day. An error of a few hours moves the
starting proportion; an error of a day changes the starting planet and shifts the entire
sequence. **This is the single strongest argument for accurate birth times**, and it is
why rectification exists as a discipline.

## Subdivisions

Each mahadasha divides into nine antardashas (bhuktis), in the same planetary order
starting from the mahadasha lord itself, with lengths proportional to the planets'
own periods. Those subdivide again into pratyantardashas, and further.

The arithmetic compounds, and it is why the computation must use exact rational or
decimal arithmetic rather than floating point: error accumulates across three levels of
subdivision and produces dates wrong by days.

## How a period is read

A dasha does not supply an event. It supplies a theme, drawn from:

- The lord's house placement and sign
- The houses the lord rules
- The lord's dignity, aspects and conjunctions
- The relationship between the mahadasha lord and the antardasha lord

A period is read as the lord's significations becoming prominent. Whether that is
welcome depends on the lord's condition — which is why the same Saturn dasha reads
entirely differently in two charts.
