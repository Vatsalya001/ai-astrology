Seven years, the first in the Vimshottari order and the shortest. Detachment, reduction
and the loosening of attachment become the organising concern.

## The theme

Classical texts read a period of letting go — things concluding, interest withdrawing
from what previously held it, and competence operating without ambition behind it. Ketu
is the body without the head: capability without appetite.

Ketu periods are frequently described in retrospect as the stretch in which the native
stopped wanting something they had wanted for years, which is the most useful
characterisation of them.

## What to read

- Ketu's house: where detachment operates, and where the native undervalues their own
  competence
- **Ketu's dispositor**, as with Rahu — Ketu owns no sign, so the lord of its sign
  carries the period's character
- The Rahu-Ketu axis as a whole, since reading one end without the other loses the
  pattern
- Any planet conjunct Ketu, read as that planet's significations operating without
  investment

## Common patterns

Ketu in the 12th is traditionally read as favourable for contemplative work. Ketu in
the 1st is read as detachment from the native's own position, which can be equanimity
or can be neglect of their own interests.

Seven years is short, and Ketu periods frequently arrive as an interval between longer
chapters — which is consistent with their nature.

## What not to say

Classical sources associate Ketu with loss, and the material should not be delivered
that way. The defensible reading is reduction rather than deprivation: the period
clears, and what remains afterwards is usually what mattered. A native told to expect
loss has been given a prediction the system does not support and cannot act on.
