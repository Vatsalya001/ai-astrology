Six years, the shortest of the nine periods after Ketu's seven.

## The theme

Identity, visibility and authority become the organising concern. Classical texts read
it as a period in which the native's own position is at issue — advancement,
recognition, or the discovery that recognition is not arriving.

The brevity matters. A six-year period is short enough that its character is usually
clear in retrospect rather than at the time, and long enough to settle a career
direction.

## What to read

- The Sun's house: where visibility is sought during the period
- The houses the Sun rules from the ascendant: Leo's house, which becomes active
- The Sun's dignity: exalted in Aries, debilitated in Libra, combust never (it is the
  source of combustion)
- Relationships with the father and with institutional authority, both solar
  significations

## Common patterns

A strong Sun in a kendra or trine is read as a period of advancement in standing. An
afflicted Sun is read not as failure but as recognition arriving in a shape the native
did not choose — a promotion into the wrong role, authority without support.

The Sun's dasha frequently coincides with questions about the father, and material
there deserves tentative framing.

## What not to say

A six-year window is not a date. A reading can say the period emphasises these
concerns; it cannot say when within it something will happen, and it must not attach a
specific outcome to a specific year.
