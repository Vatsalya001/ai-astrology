An **antardasha** — also called a **bhukti**, and the two words mean the same thing
throughout this corpus — is a sub-period within a mahadasha. A mahadasha supplies a
theme and an antardasha, or bhukti, supplies the register within it. For
the long periods — Venus at twenty years, Saturn at nineteen, Rahu at eighteen — the
sub-period is where the reading actually happens.

## How they are constructed

Each mahadasha divides into nine antardashas, in the same fixed planetary order,
**starting from the mahadasha lord itself**. So a Jupiter mahadasha begins with
Jupiter-Jupiter, then Jupiter-Saturn, Jupiter-Mercury, Jupiter-Ketu, and so on through
the cycle.

The length of each is proportional: the antardasha of planet B within the mahadasha of
planet A runs for

```
(years of A × years of B) / 120
```

So Saturn's antardasha within Jupiter's mahadasha is (16 × 19) / 120 ≈ 2.53 years.

## Why the arithmetic has to be exact

The proportions are rational numbers that rarely terminate in decimal. Float error
accumulates across mahadasha → antardasha → pratyantardasha, and by the third level it
produces dates wrong by days — which matters, because the whole purpose of the
subdivision is precision.

Exact rational or decimal arithmetic throughout is not fastidiousness; it is the
difference between a usable date and a plausible one.

## How a sub-period is read

The relationship between the two lords is the content:

- **Friends** (by the traditional friendship tables): the theme advances smoothly
- **Enemies**: the two significations work against each other, read as friction rather
  than as misfortune
- **Same planet** (Jupiter-Jupiter): the mahadasha's theme at full strength, and
  traditionally the most characteristic stretch of the whole period
- **Mutual houses**: lords in each other's 6/8 are read as a difficult sub-period
  regardless of their natural relationship

Then the usual factors — each lord's house, dignity and rulerships — applied to both.

## What this is for, and what it is not

Antardashas narrow a theme from two decades to a year or two. That is genuinely useful
for understanding a stretch of life in retrospect, and moderately useful for knowing
what a current period is emphasising.

It is **not** an event calendar. A sub-period does not cause an occurrence, and a
reading must not say what will happen or when within it. The further the subdivision
goes, the stronger the temptation to make a specific prediction and the weaker the
basis for one.
