"Strong" and "weak" are used constantly in readings and mean several different things.
These are the main ones, and they do not always agree.

## Dignity by sign

In descending order:

- **Exalted** (uccha) — strongest
- **Own sign** (swakshetra) — very strong
- **Friendly sign** — supported
- **Neutral sign**
- **Enemy sign** — weakened
- **Debilitated** (neecha) — weakest, in the sign opposite its exaltation

Each planet's exaltation and debilitation signs are in its own document. Debilitation
can be cancelled — see Neecha Bhanga, with the caution that it is over-applied.

## Directional strength (dig bala)

A separate axis, and one that often contradicts dignity:

| Planet | Strongest in house |
|---|---|
| Jupiter, Mercury | 1st |
| The Moon, Venus | 4th |
| Saturn | 7th |
| The Sun, Mars | 10th |

A debilitated Saturn in the 7th has directional strength and sign weakness
simultaneously, which is a real condition rather than a contradiction to resolve.

## Combustion

A planet within a few degrees of the Sun is read as combust and weakened. Orbs vary by
source, typically 3° to 17° depending on the planet.

Mercury is the exception worth knowing: it orbits so close to the Sun that combustion
is near its normal state, and classical texts treat it less severely on that basis.

## Retrogradation

A retrograde planet is read as strengthened by some schools and as weakened by others —
the sources genuinely disagree. The most common position is that retrogradation
intensifies a planet's effect without changing its direction, and that it adds a
quality of return or review.

Which convention is in use should be stated rather than assumed.

## Functional versus natural

A **natural** benefic is Jupiter, Venus, an unafflicted Mercury or a waxing Moon. A
**functional** benefic is whichever planet rules a favourable house **for that
ascendant** — which can be a natural malefic.

Saturn is a natural malefic and a functional benefic for Taurus and Libra ascendants,
where it rules trines. This distinction does more work in a careful reading than
natural classification does, and ignoring it is why "malefic" gets misapplied.

## The practical upshot

There is no single number for a planet's strength. The shadbala system attempts one and
is a specialist computation. For ordinary reading, check dignity, directional strength,
combustion and functional role, and note where they disagree — because the disagreement
is usually the interesting part of the chart.
