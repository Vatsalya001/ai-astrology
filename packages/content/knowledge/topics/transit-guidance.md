Transits are where the planets are **now**, read against the natal chart. They are the
time-varying layer, as against the natal chart's permanent structure and the dasha
system's long cycles.

## Counted from the Moon, not the ascendant

This is the first thing that distinguishes Vedic transit reading from the Western
convention. Classical gochara is counted from the **natal Moon's sign**, which is why
the Moon's nakshatra and sign matter so much in this system.

Many modern readers use both — from the Moon and from the ascendant — and that is a
defensible hybrid. What is not defensible is using one while quoting classical
positional rules written for the other.

## Which transits matter

In descending order of practical weight:

- **Saturn** — two and a half years per sign. The slowest and most consequential
- **Jupiter** — one year per sign. The most visible benefic transit
- **Rahu and Ketu** — eighteen months per sign, retrograde
- **Mars** — about two months per sign, and the fastest transit worth noting
- The Sun, Mercury and Venus move too quickly for anything but short-term reading
- The **Moon** changes sign every two and a half days, which is daily-horoscope
  material and should be treated with the weight that implies

## How a transit is read

The transiting planet's significations are brought to the house it is crossing,
counted from the natal Moon or ascendant. A transit does not create a condition that
is not in the natal chart; it activates one that is.

This is the most important interpretive point about transits, and the one most often
lost: **a transit is a trigger, not a cause.** Saturn crossing the 10th from the Moon
emphasises career pressure in a chart where career pressure is already indicated. In a
chart where it is not, the same transit is unremarkable.

## Dasha first, then transit

The classical priority is clear and worth following: the dasha supplies the theme, the
transit supplies the timing within it. A transit that has nothing to activate in the
current dasha is read as minor.

A reading that leads with transits — which is what most published horoscope content
does, because transits are the same for everyone — has inverted the system.

## What a transit reading must not do

Name a date for an event. Predict an occurrence. Attach a specific outcome to a
specific transit window. Transits recur for everyone on a fixed schedule; a prediction
that follows from a transit alone is a prediction about a birth cohort, not a person.
