A question about the chart itself rather than about a life area — "what does this mean",
"why is my sign different from what I thought".

## The question behind most kundli questions

**"Why does this say I'm a Taurus when I've always been told Gemini?"**

Because Vedic astrology uses the **sidereal** zodiac and most Western astrology uses
the **tropical** one. The two have drifted apart by about 24 degrees, which is roughly
24 days of solar motion — nearly a whole sign.

The drift is precession: the Earth's axis wobbles with a period of about 25,800 years,
so the equinox point moves against the fixed stars. The tropical zodiac is anchored to
the equinox; the sidereal zodiac is anchored to the stars. The correction between them
is the **ayanamsa**, and this product uses Lahiri, the Indian government standard.

Neither system is wrong. They measure from different reference points, and a native
comparing them is comparing two conventions rather than discovering an error.

## The other common questions

**"What is my Moon sign and why does it matter more here?"** Because the dasha system
is counted from the Moon's nakshatra, which makes the Moon the anchor of all timing in
this system. In Western astrology the Sun sign carries more weight; here it is one of
three primary points and not the most structural.

**"Why do I need my exact birth time?"** Because the ascendant changes every two hours
and the Moon's nakshatra changes roughly daily. An hour of error moves the ascendant,
which changes every house in the chart; a few hours can move the entire dasha sequence.
This is not fussiness — it is the difference between a chart and an approximation.

**"What is the difference between D1 and D9?"** D1 is the rasi chart, the primary one.
D9 is the navamsa, a divisional chart derived from it, traditionally read for marriage
and for the underlying strength of planets. A planet weak in D1 and strong in D9 is a
recognised and meaningful pattern.

## The register for this reading

Explanatory rather than interpretive. A kundli question is usually a request to
understand the instrument, not a request for a reading, and answering it with a
prediction misses what was asked.
