The northern Indian system scores eight dimensions out of 36 points total, based
primarily on the two natal Moons' nakshatras.

## The eight kootas

| Koota | Points | Compares |
|---|---|---|
| Varna | 1 | Spiritual compatibility by nakshatra class |
| Vashya | 2 | Mutual influence by sign |
| Tara | 3 | Nakshatra-to-nakshatra count, read for wellbeing |
| Yoni | 4 | Physical and temperamental compatibility by nakshatra animal |
| Graha Maitri | 5 | Friendship between the two Moon dispositors |
| Gana | 6 | Temperament class — Deva, Manushya, Rakshasa |
| Bhakoot | 7 | Sign-to-sign relationship, read for prosperity |
| Nadi | 8 | Constitutional compatibility |

Conventional guidance treats 18 or more out of 36 as acceptable. Bhakoot and Nadi carry
the most weight and are the two most often cited as disqualifying.

## What the system is and is not

It is a traditional screening method, designed for **arranged matches in a specific
social context** — a way to compare candidates where the parties had not met. That is
what it was built for and it does the job it was built for reasonably.

It is **not** an assessment of two people who have chosen each other. It does not model
whether they communicate, whether they want the same things, or whether they are kind
to one another, and it was never meant to.

## The hard rule

A compatibility reading must never produce a verdict. Specifically, it must not say:

- "Do not marry this person"
- "This match will not work"
- "The score is too low"

A reading that does has exceeded its evidence and taken a decision belonging to two
adults. The harm is concrete and documented: matches broken on a koota score, and
natives told they are unmarriageable.

## What is defensible

Naming dimensions that score well and dimensions that score less well, as traditional
indications, with the explanation of what each koota actually compares. "Gana koota is
low, which traditionally indicates different natural temperaments" is useful and true.
"You are incompatible" is neither.

Also worth saying: the same two charts score differently under the southern dasakoota
variants, and the systems disagree. A score is a reading within one tradition, not a
measurement.

## Mangal Dosha in a compatibility context

The classical cancellation most relevant here is that the same condition in both charts
is held to cancel. Given its roughly 50% frequency, that cancellation applies often.
Both facts belong in any mention of it.
