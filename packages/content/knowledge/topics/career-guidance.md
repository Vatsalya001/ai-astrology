What to look at, in order, when the question is about work.

## The houses

- **10th** — vocation and public standing. The primary house
- **6th** — employment and service, as distinct from vocation. Day-to-day work
- **2nd** — resources the work produces and retains
- **11th** — income from the work, and professional networks

The 10th-6th distinction does real work. A strong 10th with a weak 6th is a
recognisable pattern: a native suited to a vocation and poorly suited to being
employed in it.

## The planets

- **The 10th lord and its placement** — the single most informative factor
- **Saturn** — duration, structure, and whether effort accumulates
- **The Sun** — authority, visibility, relationship to hierarchy
- **Mercury** — the subject matter the native thinks in, and commercial capability
- The dispositor of the 10th lord, which often indicates where the career actually
  happens as against what it nominally is

## The timing

- The **current mahadasha and antardasha**, read for whether this is a period in which
  career matters are emphasised at all
- **Saturn's transit** over the 10th house and its lord — the slowest and most
  career-relevant transit
- **Jupiter's transit** over the 10th, 2nd or 11th
- **Sade Sati**, if active, which is frequently the real content of a career question
  asked at a particular moment

## What a career reading can honestly say

That the chart indicates particular arenas as more supported than others. That a
current period emphasises consolidation or change. That a specific tension exists —
between vocation and employment, between standing and satisfaction, between the 10th
and the 4th.

## What it must not say

Which job to take. Whether a specific role will work out. When a promotion will come.
Whether to resign. These depend on the employer, the market, the native's finances and
their judgement, none of which is in the chart.

The useful output of a career reading is a better-framed question for the native to
decide, not a decision made on their behalf.
