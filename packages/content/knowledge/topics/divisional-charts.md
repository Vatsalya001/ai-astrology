A divisional chart subdivides each sign into equal parts and maps those parts onto a
new set of twelve signs. The result is read as a magnification of one dimension of the
natal chart.

## The main ones

| Chart | Divisions per sign | Traditionally read for |
|---|---|---|
| **D1 — Rasi** | 1 | Everything. The primary chart |
| **D9 — Navamsa** | 9 | Marriage, and the underlying strength of planets |
| D10 — Dasamsa | 10 | Career |
| D7 — Saptamsa | 7 | Children |
| D4 — Chaturthamsa | 4 | Property and fortune |
| D3 — Drekkana | 3 | Siblings |
| D12 — Dwadasamsa | 12 | Parents |
| D16, D20, D24, D27, D30, D40, D45, D60 | as named | Various; D60 (shashtiamsa) is given great weight by some schools |

The classical set is sixteen (shodasavarga). Most practical reading uses D1 and D9,
with D10 for career questions.

## Why D9 matters most after D1

Two reasons. It is the marriage chart in nearly all schools. And it is read as the
**underlying strength** of a planet: a planet weak in D1 and strong in D9 —
vargottama, when it occupies the same sign in both — is a recognised pattern of
capacity that is not visible on the surface.

The practical rule most schools agree on: a planet's D1 placement describes what it
does, and its D9 placement describes whether it has the strength to do it.

## The precision problem

A navamsa is 3°20' of arc. Each sign contains nine of them, and the Moon crosses one in
about six hours. **Divisional charts are far more sensitive to birth-time error than
D1 is** — D60 divides each sign into 30-arcminute segments, which the ascendant crosses
in about two minutes.

This is why serious practice treats the higher divisionals with caution for any chart
whose birth time is not known to the minute, and why a reading that leans on D60 for a
rounded birth time is building on nothing.

## How to use them in a reading

D1 first and primarily. D9 to check whether a D1 conclusion is supported. D10 for a
career question where the D1 indication is ambiguous.

Not a tour of sixteen charts. The higher divisionals are a specialist tool, and
invoking them to add weight to a reading adds precision that the birth data usually
cannot support.
