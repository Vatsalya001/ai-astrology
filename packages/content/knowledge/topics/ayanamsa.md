The single number that distinguishes a Vedic chart from a Western one, and the source of
the most common confusion a native brings.

## What it is

The angular difference between the **tropical** zodiac — measured from the vernal
equinox — and the **sidereal** zodiac, measured from the fixed stars.

The two drift apart because of precession: the Earth's rotational axis wobbles with a
period of about 25,800 years, so the equinox point moves backwards against the stars at
roughly 50 arcseconds per year. Over two millennia that accumulates to about 24
degrees.

## Why the difference is nearly a whole sign

24 degrees of solar motion is about 24 days. So a native born in late May, placed in
Gemini by a tropical calculation, is frequently in Taurus sidereally. This is the whole
explanation for "my sign is different here", and it is a difference of convention
rather than an error in either system.

## Which ayanamsa this product uses

**Lahiri**, also called Chitrapaksha — the Indian government standard, adopted by the
Calendar Reform Committee and used by the national ephemeris. It is the most widely
used value in Indian practice.

Other values exist — Raman, Krishnamurti, Fagan-Bradley, True Chitra — differing by up
to about a degree. A degree is small but not negligible: it can move a planet across a
sign boundary for a birth near the cusp, and it moves nakshatra boundaries, which moves
the dasha sequence.

This is why the ayanamsa is recorded with every stored chart and included in every
cache key. A chart computed under one ayanamsa and read under another is a different
chart, and the difference is invisible unless the value is carried with the data.

## What to tell a native

That their sign differs because the two systems measure from different reference
points. That precession is the cause and it is well understood astronomically. That
neither system is wrong — they are different conventions — and that Vedic astrology
has used the sidereal frame throughout its history.

Not that the Western system is mistaken. That is both unnecessary and false.
