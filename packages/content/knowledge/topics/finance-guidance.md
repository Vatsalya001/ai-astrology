## The houses, and the distinction that matters

- **2nd** — wealth retained. What the native holds
- **11th** — income received. What comes in
- **5th** — speculation, and merit that makes resources available
- **9th** — fortune in the sense of what is given rather than earned

**The 2nd-11th distinction is the first thing to check**, before any yoga:

- Strong 11th, weak 2nd — money arrives and does not stay
- Strong 2nd, weak 11th — resources consolidate but do not grow
- Both strong — the classical reading of durable wealth
- Both weak — read as structure rather than quantity

This does more work in a practical reading than every dhana yoga combined.

## The planets

- The 2nd and 11th lords, and any connection between them
- **Jupiter** — wealth as distinct from income
- **Venus** — what the native considers worth spending on
- **Saturn** in the 11th, traditionally a strong placement for income that accumulates
  slowly and does not reverse

## Timing

The dashas of the 2nd and 11th lords, and Jupiter's transit over the 2nd, 11th or 5th.

## The hard limits

A reading must not:

- Promise wealth, or name an amount
- Indicate timing for a financial gain or loss
- Advise on a specific investment, business or transaction
- Report a daridra (poverty) yoga — see that document for why

Financial outcome depends on economy, inheritance, education, health and choice to a
degree no chart addresses. The classical sources were written where material position
was far more fixed at birth than it now is, and their confidence does not transfer.

## What is defensible

Whether the chart indicates resources consolidate readily or with effort. Where earning
capacity is indicated. What the native's relationship to retention looks like. Whether
the current period emphasises accumulation or expenditure.

That is genuinely useful, and it is the limit.
