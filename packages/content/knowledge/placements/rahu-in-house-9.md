The ninth is the most auspicious house in the classical scheme, and Rahu is the
significator of departure from the inherited. This is therefore the placement where the
texts are most visibly divided, and the division is the useful part of the reading.

## Where the sources disagree

One line treats a node in the ninth as friction with received belief — distance from
the father, from the family's religion, from a teacher's authority. Another treats it as
foreign higher learning and long-distance travel, which the ninth already signifies and
Rahu intensifies. These are not two phrasings of one claim; they are different readings,
and the classical literature does not adjudicate between them.

The defensible synthesis is narrow: a philosophy arrived at rather than inherited.
Whether the arrival involved a break is a question for the native and not for the chart.

## What it is traditionally read as

Higher study pursued outside the expected channel; interest in doctrines the family did
not hold; travel that is formative rather than recreational; a relationship to
principle that has been argued with rather than accepted. The ninth is a trine, so the
amplification has somewhere to go — unconventional merit, which is the phrase the
tradition keeps returning to for a node in a dharma house.

## Father and teacher

Both significations sit in this house and the sources blur them. With Rahu here the
pull toward a verdict about the father is strong and should be refused. The reading is
of voluntary authority that was not accepted uncritically, and that may describe a
teacher, a father, both, or a tradition.

## The caution

Conviction arrived at late tends to be held tightly. The tradition pairs this placement
with certainty that outruns its evidence — worth naming precisely because it is the one
thing the native is least placed to notice.
