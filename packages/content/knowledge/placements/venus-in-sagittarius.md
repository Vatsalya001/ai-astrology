## A neutral sign, read from one side only

Sagittarius belongs to Jupiter, and Venus counts Jupiter neutral, so this is a
neutral-sign placement. The relationship is not symmetric, though: Jupiter's own
friendship list counts Venus an enemy. Natural friendship (naisargika maitri) is
directional in the classical scheme, and most modern tables quietly symmetrise it.
Venus and Jupiter are the clearest surviving example of the asymmetry.

## Why the asymmetry has practical consequences

It decides which dignity you are entitled to use. Venus in Sagittarius sits in a
neutral sign; Jupiter in Taurus or Libra sits in an enemy's. The same pair of planets,
two different answers, depending on whose position is being assessed. A reading that
applies one symmetrical table to both gets one of the two wrong, and the error is
invisible because the output still looks like a dignity.

## Dual fire on appetite

Sagittarius contributes fire aimed at a principle, with a dual modality that can change
target without changing nature. Venus here is traditionally read as appetite
subordinated to a conviction: beauty valued for what it means rather than for how it
looks, attachment framed as shared belief, generosity that is principled before it is
personal. The liability the sign's literature names — the particular case failing to
fit the principle — shows up as attachment measured against a standard.

## Honest ranking

By dignity alone this is unremarkable. Sagittarius holds no exaltation or debilitation
among the classical seven, so a neutral sign with no dignity landmarks is a placement
whose interest lies in register rather than in strength. Nothing in it forecasts
anything about a relationship, a belief, or a journey.
