The short version is already stated elsewhere in this corpus: Jupiter in the sixth
expands what the house signifies, and that is not a gift. What follows is why the
classical sources nonetheless do not agree about it.

## Dusthana and upachaya at once

The sixth is both, and the two rules address different planets. Malefics strengthen
here because the house is resistance and they supply it. Benefics receive no equivalent
bonus — the asymmetry is in the rule, not an oversight in it. The sixth's natural sign
is Virgo, ruled by Mercury, whom Jupiter counts an enemy, which is a second small mark
in the natural zodiac.

## Where the texts genuinely disagree

One line of tradition reads this placement as debt and litigation that grow: generous
obligation, opposition with resources behind it. Another reads it as protection within
difficulty — the chart's most protective planet stationed in the house of illness and
enemies — and associates it with healing work and with service that is valued rather
than merely performed. Neither position dominates the classical material. The honest
reading holds both: the arena is enlarged, and so is whatever meets it.

## Service becoming vocation

From the sixth, Jupiter aspects the tenth, twelfth and second. The tenth is the useful
one. Daily discipline and employment reaching into vocation is the traditional basis for
reading this placement in physicians, administrators, and people whose career is other
people's problems.

## The caution

This placement is reported as misfortune far more often than the sources support, and
it occurs in roughly one chart in twelve — frequency worth stating before any weight is
put on it. On health it says nothing diagnostic: it describes a traditional pattern of
vulnerability, and does not identify a condition, anticipate an illness, or substitute
for a physician.
