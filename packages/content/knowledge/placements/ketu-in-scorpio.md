## The one sign some schools hand to Ketu

This is the most argued-over of the twelve. Classical sources give Scorpio to Mars and
give Ketu nothing, but several modern schools assign Ketu co-rulership here, and the same
schools often call it Ketu's exaltation. So Scorpio is the only sign where a reading
might legitimately describe Ketu as being at home — and the description depends entirely
on a convention adopted after the classical texts, which should be stated as such rather
than slipped in as dignity.

## The axis where the modern claims stack

Rahu is necessarily in Taurus, which the same conventions treat as Rahu's own strongest
sign. This one axis is therefore the place where a modern scheme's two largest claims
land simultaneously, and that coincidence is most of why this placement is written about
more than its neighbours. Under the competing Gemini-and-Sagittarius scheme, the axis is
unremarkable. Both cannot be right.

## Fixed water, worked at continuously

Mars remains the classical dispositor, and in Scorpio its force is applied steadily
rather than in bursts. Ketu's significations — capability without appetite, interest in
what is beneath the stated version — are read as agreeing with that rather than
straining against it. This is where the traditional association of Ketu with
investigation, with hidden subjects and with contemplative practice is usually traced.
Note also that the Moon falls in Scorpio, which matters considerably if the Moon is the
co-tenant.

## The over-sold reading

Popular writing turns this placement into a declaration of spiritual attainment. The
tradition is more modest: agreement between a significator and a sign describes where
something operates easily, not whether it operates well, and not what the native does
with it. No urgency attaches to it and nothing needs to be bought on its account.
