Sagittarius is the ninth sign and Jupiter rules it. The ninth house is dharma bhava and
Jupiter is the significator of dharma. Nothing else in the planet-and-house grid lines
up this completely, and the classical sources respond with near-unanimous praise.

## Unanimity is itself a caution

When the texts stop disagreeing they also stop distinguishing. The descriptions of this
placement are superlatives with very little mechanism in them, and they are not more
reliable for being consistent. The specifics that survive are narrower: a native given
more than they arranged for, in matters of learning, travel, doctrine and the father.

Dignity still belongs to the sign. Jupiter occupies its own sign in the ninth house only
for an Aries ascendant — the rest is the natural zodiac's resonance, not every chart's.

## Not a mahapurusha yoga

The ninth is a trine, not a kendra, so Jupiter exalted or in its own sign here forms no
Pancha Mahapurusha yoga at all. The strongest-looking Jupiter in a chart can be the one
with no named yoga attached to it, which is worth saying before a reading treats yoga
inventory as a strength measure.

## The teacher twice over

Jupiter is the guru and the ninth is the house of the teacher, so the signification
doubles rather than broadens. Traditionally read as a native expected to hold the
position: consulted on principle, assumed to have a view, asked before they have
decided. The texts are notably silent on what that expectation costs, and a careful
reading supplies what they omit.

## The caution

A supported ninth is read as difficulty arriving with resources attached, not as
difficulty avoided. The reservation specific to Jupiter here is doctrine held past its
evidence — conviction inherited rather than tested, and generosity extended on
principle to people who have not earned it.
