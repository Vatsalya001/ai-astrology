Scorpio is the Moon's debilitation — neecha — its only one, with the exact point at
3° Scorpio. This is the one placement in this set that classical sources treat as a
significant condition rather than a detail.

## What the debilitation involves, and what it does not

Debilitation is a dignity term and here it stands alone: Mars is neutral toward the
Moon, not hostile. So this is weakness of dignity without compounding enmity, which
distinguishes it from debilitations where lord and occupant are also enemies. Fixed
water is traditionally read as holding rather than releasing, and the Moon's
significations — receptivity, emotional movement, what is taken in and let go — are
precisely what cannot circulate there.

## Neecha bhanga: the classical cancellation

The texts provide for debilitation to be cancelled. The conditions usually cited for
the Moon in Scorpio are the dispositor Mars, or Venus as lord of the Moon's exaltation
sign, occupying a kendra — first, fourth, seventh or tenth — from the ascendant or from
the Moon. Sources differ on the exact list and on how complete the cancellation is. A
reading should check the condition rather than assume either the weakness or its
removal.

## How common this is

About one birth in twelve. A debilitated Moon is an ordinary chart feature, not a rare
affliction, and treating it as a calamity misstates both the frequency and the
classical position.

## The reading, and its limit

Depth, retention, and reluctance to disclose are the standard readings; difficulty
letting something go is the standard caution. None of it is a statement about anyone's
health or circumstances, and material touching early emotional life should be offered
as something to recognise or set aside, never as a finding.
