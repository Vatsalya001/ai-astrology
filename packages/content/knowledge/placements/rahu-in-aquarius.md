Aquarius is the only sign in which Rahu is sometimes given rulership rather than merely
a dignity, which makes it the one most worth being careful about. Some modern schools
assign Rahu co-rulership of Aquarius alongside Saturn. The classical scheme does not:
Saturn rules both of its signs, and the nodes rule nothing.

## What a co-rulership claim is worth

Treat it as the nearest thing to an own sign and still not an own sign. Co-rulership is
a modern convenience that lets an interpreter say something about a node without
reaching for the dispositor, and the dispositor read — Saturn, friendly to Rahu, in
whatever condition the chart actually gives it — remains the defensible one. A reading
must not announce Rahu as "in its own sign" here.

## Structure applied to ideas

Aquarius is fixed air: Saturn's discipline operating on abstractions rather than things,
concerned with the collective rather than the individual. Rahu's own significations fit
this sign more naturally than any other — technology, the unfamiliar, people outside the
inherited circle, systems that do not exist yet. The placement is traditionally read as
appetite for scale: networks, movements, large groups, the engineering of something
impersonal.

## The caution

Fixed air holds its positions for structural reasons and does not warm to the particular
case. Rahu amplifies. The traditional reading is a cause pursued past the people inside
it, and a certainty about the system that is not available about any one member of it.
Shared across an eighteen-month cohort; the house is what narrows it.
