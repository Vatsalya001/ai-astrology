## The dignity

Enemy sign. Mars rules Scorpio and is one of Saturn's enemies; the Moon is debilitated
here and nothing is exalted. Some schools assign Ketu co-rulership, which genuinely
complicates the assessment, because Saturn's relationship to the nodes is not part of the
classical friendship scheme at all. A reading that leans on co-rulership should say that
it is doing so.

## Agreement on method, disagreement on motive

The most interesting of the three enemy placements. Saturn and Scorpio want the same
behaviour — persistence, patience, not disclosing — because fixed water holds and
Saturn's entire instrument is duration. What they disagree about is motive: Mars drives
towards something, Saturn restrains. So this is not read as friction in the way Saturn in
Leo is. It is read as considerable staying power pointed at something the native does not
discuss.

## What it is read as doing

Significations of investigation, research and work done out of sight take on Saturn's
timescale. The classical emphasis is endurance under sustained pressure — attrition
rather than confrontation. Texts also describe a capacity to hold a grievance for a long
time, which is Mars's intensity lent Saturn's memory.

## Reading it responsibly

Scorpio is the sign where the Moon is at its weakest, and adding a malefic to a sign with
that reputation reliably produces overstated readings: the eighth-house vocabulary of
crisis, secrecy and loss gets attached regardless of which house Saturn actually
occupies. The sign is not the house. What this placement supports is a claim about how
the native works, and nothing at all about what happens to them.
