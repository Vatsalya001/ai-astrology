## Jupiter again, and not the same reading

Pisces shares its lord with Sagittarius, so the dispositor is Jupiter here too, and the
nodes again have no dignity of their own to assess. The two placements are not
interchangeable. In Sagittarius, Jupiter governs principle and aim; in Pisces it governs
dissolution, the end of the circle, the point at which distinctions stop holding. A
significator of release in a sign about release is a different proposition from one in a
sign about conclusions.

## The most over-written of the twelve

Popular material treats Ketu in Pisces as an announcement of spiritual advancement,
usually by reading its moksha signification twice — once from the planet and once from
the sign. Classical sources are considerably more restrained. Where a planet's nature and
its sign agree, the tradition says the expression is unobstructed; it does not say the
expression is good, and it certainly does not say anything about the native's attainment.

## Dual water at the end of the circle

Pisces is diffuse and does not insist on its own boundaries. Ketu is traditionally read
as not wanting. Together they are read as a loosened grip on a sign that was not gripping
much — which presents as devotion and absorption in some charts and as drift and
unavailability in others. The placement does not adjudicate between them, and no part of
it is read as a statement about health, means or anyone's capacity to manage their
affairs. Venus exalts and Mercury falls in Pisces, both of which concern co-tenants
rather than the node.

## Rahu in Virgo

The opposite end supplies the contrast that makes this legible: appetite for method,
correctness and the checked detail, with meaning treated as already settled. Named from
one side only, the pattern reads as half of itself.
