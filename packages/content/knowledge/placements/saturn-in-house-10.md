This complements the shorter entry on the same placement, which covers the occupations
traditionally associated with it, public standing, and timing. None of that is repeated
here. What follows is the structural account: why the placement is strong, and one way
it is routinely claimed to be strong that it is not.

## Three strengths, and one that is not there

The tenth is a kendra and an upachaya house — the only house that is both — and in the
natural zodiac it is Capricorn, Saturn's own sign. That stacking is why the placement
has the reputation it has, and the reputation is deserved.

It does not, however, carry directional strength. Dig bala in the tenth belongs to the
Sun and to Mars; Saturn's dig bala house is the seventh. The claim that Saturn is
directionally strong in the tenth is widespread and wrong, and it matters because it is
used to upgrade a reading that was already strong on other grounds. Strength is not more
persuasive for being counted twice.

## Own sign, with a caveat

The Capricorn correspondence is a natural-zodiac resonance. It is literal only for an
Aries ascendant; for every other rising sign, the sign actually occupying the tenth
governs dignity, and it may be one in which Saturn is debilitated. Checking the real
sign before invoking own-sign strength is the whole of the discipline here.

## What it aspects, and what it does not

Saturn in the tenth aspects the twelfth, fourth and seventh — not the first. The fourth
is the consequential one: career pressure falling on the house of the home. That is the
fourth-tenth tension arriving from the vocational side rather than the domestic one.
