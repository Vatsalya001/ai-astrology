## Two candidate dispositors, and a convention to declare

Classically Aquarius is Saturn's, and Saturn is the dispositor through which Ketu — which
rules no sign and holds no dignity — has to be read. Several modern schools, though,
assign Aquarius co-rulership to Rahu. Under that convention this placement is a node
lodged in the sign of the other end of its own axis, which is a genuinely different
reading from the classical one. Aquarius is one of only two signs where this ambiguity
arises, Scorpio being the other, and the two are mirror cases. State which lordship is
in use; do not average them.

## Impersonal twice over

Aquarius already operates on principle rather than preference, and already treats the
particular person as a case rather than a claim. Ketu adds absence of appetite to a sign
that was not asking for much. The result is traditionally read as the least frictional
of the twelve, not because it is beneficial but because planet and sign are not pulling
against each other — there is no characteristic tension here of the kind Taurus or Leo
supplies. Aquarius also carries no exaltation or debilitation among the seven, so there
is no dignity contrast to draw on either.

## Reading it from the Leo end

Rahu is necessarily in Leo. The pattern is usually clearer from there: appetite for
recognition and individual standing, set against competence inside the group that the
native does not count. Shatabhisha, Rahu's asterism, occupies mid-Aquarius, so the node
can sit in its counterpart's territory.

## Where this gets overstated

Writing about this placement tends toward claims about detachment from society or
indifference to belonging. The configuration supports a quieter statement: the sign's own
distance from the personal, with the significator of non-attachment in it.
