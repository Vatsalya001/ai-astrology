The Moon in the sixth house is the placement where the sixth house's best-known rule
works against the planet rather than for it.

## The rule that helps Saturn does not help the Moon

The sixth is a dusthana and an upachaya house at once, and malefics are read as
strengthened in it: a strong planet in the house of obstacles reads as strength against
them rather than strength of them. That argument was built for Mars and Saturn. A
waxing Moon is a natural benefic, and a benefic in the sixth expands what the house
signifies, which is not a gift. Classical sources rate this among the weaker Moon
placements for precisely that reason.

A waning Moon muddies the picture, since most sources class it as weak or malefic and
some of the upachaya logic then becomes arguable. The texts do not develop the case,
and it is better named as open than settled.

## The useful reading

Mind engaged with resistance. A sixth-house Moon is traditionally read as emotional
attention absorbed by opposition, debt, routine and service — work done for someone
else on a daily discipline. Sources note capacity for sustained unglamorous effort, and
an emotional life that organises itself around problems to be managed.

The sixth governs employment as distinct from vocation. Emotional investment in a job
rather than in a calling is a recognisable version of this.

## On health

The sixth governs illness and the Moon governs the body's fluid, receptive side, which
makes this the placement most likely to attract a medical claim. An astrological
indication is not a diagnosis and must never be offered as one. The tradition describes
a pattern of sensitivity in traditional terms; it does not name a condition, and a
physician is the only appropriate destination for the question.
