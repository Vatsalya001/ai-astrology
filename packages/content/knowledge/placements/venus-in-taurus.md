## Own sign, but not the moolatrikona

Taurus is one of Venus's two own signs (swakshetra) — strong dignity, ranked below
exaltation and above everything else. The distinction worth holding on to is that the
moolatrikona portion belongs to Libra, not here. Taurus is own-sign across all thirty
degrees and nothing more, which makes it the simpler of Venus's two home placements to
assess: there is no sub-boundary to check.

## Earth and fixity: significations at their most literal

Venus in its own earthy sign is traditionally read through its tangible significations
rather than its abstract ones — land, vehicles, food, jewellery, the physical crafts,
comfort of the kind one can sit on. Fixity adds duration: appetite that stays with its
object. The sources read this as reliable taste, and they are candid about the other
face of the same property, which is taste that does not revise. Where the situation has
changed and the preference has not, the strength and the liability are one thing.

## An enemy exalted in Venus's own house

The Moon is exalted in Taurus, and Venus counts the Moon an enemy. Venus's own sign
hosts its enemy's maximum dignity — the exact inverse of Libra, where a friend is
exalted instead. A Venus–Moon conjunction here is therefore a strong Venus sharing a
sign with a strong planet it is natively unfriendly to. The classical treatment is that
both are strong at once and the tension is real, not that one cancels the other.

## Malavya, and what it does not settle

In a kendra, this placement satisfies the Malavya condition, which has its own document
and its own caveats. It is support for a question about aesthetic or relational
capacity, not an answer to one, and it settles nothing about a marriage.
