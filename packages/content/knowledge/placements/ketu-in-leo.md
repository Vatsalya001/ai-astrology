## The dispositor is nearly the whole reading

Leo carries no exaltation and no debilitation among the seven planets, and the nodes
appear in no dignity table at all. Two absences compound: there is no sign-dignity to
assess for Ketu, and the sign itself offers no dignity-based handle. What remains is the
Sun as lord of Leo, and its house, strength and freedom from affliction. Of the twelve
Ketu signs, this is the one where the dispositor carries the largest share of the
judgement.

## Magha

Ketu rules Magha, 0° to 13°20' of Leo — the asterism of the ancestors. A Ketu in early
Leo is therefore a node standing in its own territory inside the sign of the self, which
is the combination classical commentators actually develop at length. The reading
usually offered is inheritance that arrives without being sought, and standing held by
descent rather than by acquisition. Roughly the first forty-four percent of the sign is
Magha, so this is common.

## Indifference in the sign that wants acknowledgement

Leo's characteristic concern is whether the native's position is recognised. Ketu's
significations are the opposite motion: capability without appetite for its reward. The
contradiction is the placement, and texts make something of it — traditionally read as
authority exercised without interest in the credit, or as a reluctance to claim what has
in fact been earned. Which of those a chart supports is decided elsewhere in it.

## Combustion is a different question

Where the Sun shares the sign, node and luminary are conjunct and the eclipse reading
applies. That is not combustion, which is about a planet losing visibility near the Sun
and does not transfer to a point with no body. Keeping the two separate avoids stacking
two weaknesses where the tradition names one configuration.
