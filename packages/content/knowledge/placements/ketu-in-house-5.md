A trine, and the house of what the native produces. Ketu in a dharma house is read more
favourably than Ketu in a kendra, and the fifth's particular business — output brought
into being and then not fully controlled — sits unusually well with a planet that
produces without wanting the result.

## Magha

Ketu rules Magha, the opening 13°20' of Leo and so of the natural fifth sign. The
nakshatra's deities are the Pitris, the ancestors, and where Ketu in the fifth falls
there the reading takes on a specific colour: ability that feels inherited rather than
developed, and creative authority the native treats as borrowed. This is the clearest
case of Ketu's nakshatra rulerships adding something a house reading alone would miss.

## Creative work that is not claimed

The usual report is competence in a craft the native does not call theirs — writing,
performance, teaching, design done well and described as something they happen to do.
Naming it is the useful move in a reading, because the fifth is where undervalued skill
goes unexercised rather than merely unnoticed.

## Children — the boundary

This is the most emotionally loaded thing a chart gets asked about, and the classical
literature is more confident on Ketu in the fifth than is defensible; some sources read
it as denial of children outright. That reading is not reproduced here and must not be
offered. Whether a native has children involves medicine, circumstance and choice, and
no placement is a prognosis. What can be said is traditional and conditional: that the
house is supported or stressed, and that the native's relationship to the matter may be
easy or effortful.

## A caution on speculation

The fifth also governs games of chance. Ketu here is not read as luck in them —
detachment from an outcome is not an edge, and a reading must not imply that it is.
