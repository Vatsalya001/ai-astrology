The Moon is exalted in Taurus — uchcha — and this is the strongest position the Moon
holds by sign anywhere in the zodiac.

## Exaltation, and a moolatrikona outside the Moon's own sign

The classical scheme places exact exaltation at 3° Taurus and the Moon's moolatrikona
in the remainder, 4° to 30°. That makes the Moon the one case among the seven planets
where the moolatrikona sign and the own sign come apart: it rules Cancer but takes its
moolatrikona strength in Taurus. The practical consequence is that essentially the
whole sign is strong ground for the Moon, which is not true of any other planet's
exaltation sign.

Exact exaltation covers three degrees of 360, so it is genuinely uncommon — under one
percent of births. Exaltation by sign is not uncommon at all: roughly one birth in
twelve.

## Rohini

Rohini, 10°00' to 23°20' Taurus, is the Moon's own nakshatra. A Moon there occupies its
exaltation sign and its own nakshatra at once, which classical sources read as among
the better positions available to it.

## Venus is not a friend here

The assumption usually runs the other way, so it is worth stating: Venus counts the
Moon an enemy in the natural-relationship table, while the Moon counts Venus neutral.
The Moon's best sign by dignity is ruled by a planet hostile to it. Exaltation is a
property of the sign, not a courtesy from its lord.

## What exaltation does and does not promise

Strength, not virtue. A strong Moon in fixed earth is traditionally read as steady
feeling that is also hard to move, and the caution the texts attach is attachment
rather than instability. Exaltation describes capacity; capacity is spent in whatever
direction the rest of the chart points.
