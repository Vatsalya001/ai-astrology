The Moon in the eleventh house is among the better-regarded Moon placements in the
classical literature. The reasons usually given for that are worth separating from the
reasons that hold.

## Why it is rated well, and on what grounds

The eleventh is labha bhava, the house of gains and of networks, and it is an upachaya
house. Sources read a Moon here as income arriving through people — work that depends
on being liked, and support that appears because the native is connected rather than
because they asked for it.

The upachaya argument that strengthens Saturn in this house does not transfer to a
waxing Moon, which needs no help from it. What does hold is simpler and weaker: a
benefic in the house of gains, with nothing structural working against it.

## Friendship as a resource

The eleventh describes networks as a resource; the Moon describes emotional attachment.
The combination is read as a native whose emotional life is substantially located in
friendships and groups rather than in the household — and who therefore feels the state
of those friendships the way others feel the state of their home.

Elder siblings belong to this house as well, and sources read closeness to them as part
of the same pattern.

## A caution

The eleventh grants what is wanted without reference to whether it was worth wanting,
and classical sources pair it with Rahu for exactly that reason. A Moon here is read as
desire felt strongly and pursued through people, which is not the same as desire well
chosen.

Income is not wealth — the second house governs what is kept — and nothing in this
placement supports a statement about either. A strong eleventh also says nothing about
whether the native's friendships are good, only that they are consequential.
