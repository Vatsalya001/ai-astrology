Of the six Mangal Dosha positions this is the one the literature is actually about, and
the one where restraint does the most work.

## Frequency, stated first

Mars occupies any given house in roughly one chart in twelve, and the Mangal Dosha
condition as a whole covers about half of all charts by construction. The classical
texts list extensive cancellations, including the same condition in a partner's chart. A
reading that treats this placement as a verdict has misread both its frequency and its
own literature. Frequency belongs at the front of the reading, not as a softening added
after the alarm.

## Neither dignity nor direction

The seventh holds Libra in the natural zodiac, Venus's sign, where Mars is neutral —
neither dignified nor debilitated. Directional strength in the seventh belongs to
Saturn, not to Mars, so this placement gains nothing from dig bala either. By every
strength axis it is ordinary; its entire reputation comes from signification.

## What is defensibly read

Kalatra bhava is whatever the self meets as an equal and cannot absorb. Mars there is
traditionally read as a native who engages a partner head-on: initiative in forming the
partnership, impatience inside it, disagreement conducted openly rather than withheld.
The sources describe heat, and heat is not distinguishable from the placement alone into
passion or into friction.

## Business partnership, the least fraught reading

The seventh governs partnership of every kind, and the commercial case carries none of
the emotional load: Mars there is read as a partner who drives, which suits a venture
needing momentum and strains one needing consensus.

## What must not be said

No statement that a native will or will not marry, that a marriage will or will not
last, or that a particular person is unsuitable. Compatibility work can name tensions
and strengths and nothing further. Here more than anywhere, delivering a verdict is
exactly the misuse the tradition is best known for.
