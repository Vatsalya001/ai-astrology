The fourth is the nadir — what cannot be seen from outside at all — and it is the house
of the emotional base. Placing the planet of withdrawn attachment at the chart's lowest
and most private point is, in classical terms, among the harder things Ketu does. The
third house makes it an asset. The fourth does not.

## What the texts actually say

Bandhu bhava is the house of connection, and Ketu is read as connection the native does
not reach for. Sources describe a home left early, a sense of not being from where one
is from, or a residence held without being settled into. They are more consistent on
this placement than on most nodal material.

## Directional strength, and its absence

The Moon and Venus hold dig bala in the fourth, which is why the house is read for
emotional security through the Moon in particular. The nodes appear nowhere in the
directional strength table — the classical scheme assigns dig bala to the seven grahas
only. That silence is worth naming rather than filling: Ketu in the fourth is neither
directionally strong nor directionally weak, and claims otherwise are modern.

## The axis across the meridian

Rahu is in the tenth by construction, and this is the most legible of the six nodal
axes: appetite in public standing, indifference toward the private base that would
sustain it. The fourth house document names that tension in general; the axis sharpens
it into a question to ask rather than a verdict to deliver.

## Where to tread carefully

Material about the mother and early home life is the likeliest thing in a chart to touch
something undiscussed. Offer it tentatively, leave room to be wrong, and do not press
when a native declines it.
