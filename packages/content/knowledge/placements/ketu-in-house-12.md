Vyaya bhava is the house classical texts associate with moksha, and Ketu is the planet
they associate with it. This is the one placement in the set where planet and house
signify the same thing, and it is the most unqualified endorsement the classical
literature gives Ketu anywhere.

## Why the endorsement is narrower than it sounds

The twelfth is also the house of expenditure and the last of the three dusthanas, and
the classical sources do not resolve that tension by choosing the pleasant meaning. They
treat the house as costly and treat the spiritual reading as what becomes available once
the cost is accepted. Ketu in the twelfth inherits both halves. Read as contemplative
capacity it is well supported; read as a guarantee of spiritual progress it is not
supported at all, and that is the commonest error made with this placement.

## Solitude, and the two kinds of it

Isolation and confinement are twelfth-house significations, and Ketu is read as a native
who needs less company than most and notices its absence late. The distinction that
matters in a reading is between solitude chosen and solitude arrived at. Nothing in the
placement makes that distinction — the first and fourth houses and the native's own
account do.

## Residence that separates

The twelfth is living apart from one's origin, as distinct from the ninth's journey
undertaken. Ketu here is read as leaving without ceremony and without homesickness. It
is one of the few significations in this document that is practically checkable.

## Loss, and how not to say it

Rahu sits in the sixth, putting appetite into opposition, debt and daily struggle — a
native drawn toward a fight while withdrawing from the world is a real and uncomfortable
pattern, and naming both ends is the point. The twelfth's loss signification must never
be delivered as fear. Reduction is the defensible word; deprivation is not.
