The sixth is a dusthana and an upachaya at once, and for a malefic the upachaya
classification wins. Classical texts are unusually positive about a sixth-house Sun:
read as strength against opposition rather than as affliction, and listed among the
better placements for it.

## Strength measured against something

The reading is adversarial by construction. Competition, litigation, institutional
opposition, and the discipline of daily work are the arenas, and a Sun here is
traditionally read as holding position in them — few enemies who remain effective, and
a native who does not fold under a formal challenge. This is the house where the word
"malefic" is least informative about the outcome.

## Employment, not vocation

The sixth is service; the tenth is vocation. The distinction does real work here. A
sixth-house Sun is traditionally read as authority exercised inside a structure
someone else owns — the senior position in an institution rather than the name over
the door. Many natives find that entirely satisfactory, and a reading that treats it
as a thwarted tenth house has imported an assumption the tradition does not make.

## On health, the limit

The sixth governs illness, and the standard lists attach heat, the stomach and the
eyes to a Sun placed here. Those are traditional significator associations and nothing
more. They do not name a condition, do not predict one, and do not survive contact
with a physician, which is where any symptom belongs.

## The caution

The strength of this placement needs an opponent. The traditional caution, stated
plainly, is a native who manufactures conflict in order to have something to prevail
over — and who then reads the resulting friction as proof the reading was right.
