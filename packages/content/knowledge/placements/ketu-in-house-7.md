A kendra, and the house most often asked about. It is also the placement where stating
frequency does more work than any interpretive subtlety available.

## Start with how common this is

Roughly one chart in twelve has Ketu in the seventh — about eight percent of everyone —
and the popular literature treats the placement as close to disqualifying for marriage.
Both of those cannot be true. Establishing frequency comes before assigning weight, and
it is the single most useful correction a reading can make on this subject.

## The geometry

The seventh is whatever the self meets as an equal and cannot absorb, and Rahu is
necessarily in the first. The axis reads as appetite located in one's own position and
withdrawn from the counterparty's separateness — a tendency to under-register that the
other person is a different person. Classical sources describe reserve within
partnership far more often than absence of partnership.

## Business, where the freight is lighter

Partnership in trade belongs to this house as much as marriage does, and the nodal
material applies there with much less emotional loading: agreements entered without
heavy investment in the other side's interests, and a readiness to walk away that can be
discipline or can be carelessness.

## No verdict is available

A reading must not state that a native will or will not marry, that a marriage will or
will not last, or that a particular person is unsuitable. Compatibility scoring can name
tensions and strengths; it cannot deliver a judgement on two people who have chosen each
other, and Ketu in the seventh is not grounds for one.

## What natives actually report

Many natives with this placement report long settled partnerships and describe
themselves as undemanding within them. A reading that arrives expecting difficulty will
find it, and should not.
