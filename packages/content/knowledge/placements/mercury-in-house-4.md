## A sign Mercury does not get on with

Cancer is the fourth's natural sign and the Moon's own, and the classical friendship
scheme makes the Moon Mercury's one outright enemy. This is the only kendra where that
enmity lands by natural correspondence. Where an ascendant does place Cancer on the
fourth, Mercury is read as working in unfriendly medium — the faculty of distinction
operating in the element of feeling, which the texts treat as diluting to both. Elsewhere
read the sign actually there.

## Analysis turned inward

The fourth sits at the nadir, and the house document is explicit that this geometry is
its whole meaning: the tenth is what can be seen from a distance, the fourth is what
cannot be seen at all. Mercury here is traditionally read as a mind that works unobserved
— private study, self-education, a thinking life conducted where nobody is watching.
Classical sources also attach land and property dealings to the placement, since the
fourth holds immovable property and Mercury holds the negotiation of it.

## The specific substitution

An analytical faculty in the house of emotional foundation is read as a native who
examines their base rather than rests on it. The traditional framing for that is
self-knowledge. The honest framing is that describing a feeling and having one are
different operations, and the fourth is the house where substituting the first for the
second is easiest and least visible.

## Handling the material

The fourth-house reading already asks for tentative framing on the mother and on early
home life. Mercury here raises the stakes, because it is read as a native with ready
words for that history — which makes a reading land as more confirmed than its evidence
warrants. Leave room to be wrong.

## Directional note

Dig bala in the fourth belongs to the Moon and Venus. Mercury's is in the first, so this
placement carries angular weight without directional strength.
