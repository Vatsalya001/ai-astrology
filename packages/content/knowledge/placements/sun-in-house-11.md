An upachaya house, so the Sun strengthens here, and the classical verdict is
straightforwardly favourable — one of the easier solar placements to read, with less
argument attached to it than the ninth or the seventh.

## Inflow that follows from position

Three houses carry money and they are not interchangeable: the tenth is standing, the
second is what is retained, the eleventh is what arrives. A Sun here is traditionally
read as income arising from position — title, office, seniority, a recognised role —
rather than from trade or from speculation. The practical signature is remuneration
that tracks rank rather than volume.

## Among a group, and not of it

The eleventh governs friends, networks and large organisations, and this is where the
placement gets specific. The Sun among a native's circle is traditionally read as the
figure others defer to, and the same reading inverted gives the difficulty: being an
ordinary member of anything is the part that chafes. Elder siblings are an eleventh
signification too, and classical sources pair that with the Sun as a relationship where
authority is contested rather than absent.

## Never combust, never retrograde

Worth stating once in a placement series, and this is the house where it bites. The Sun
is the source of combustion, so it cannot be combust, and it does not retrograde. Its
condition therefore rests on only three things: the sign it occupies, the house, and
what it burns by sharing a house with. An eleventh-house reading that wants more
nuance has to come from the sign, not from the planet's own state.

## The caution

This describes inflow, not amount. Nothing here supports a statement about how much a
native will have, or when — and the eleventh's reputation for granting what is wanted
is not a reason to make one.
