Three classifications line up behind the same placement here, which happens nowhere
else in the solar series. The tenth is a kendra, it is an upachaya where a malefic
strengthens, and it is the Sun's house of directional strength. That convergence is
why the classical texts are more confident about this combination than about any other
position of the Sun.

## An occupancy strength, which is the exception

The usual rule for the tenth is that the lord's condition matters more than the
occupants. The Sun is the exception most often cited against it, because dig bala is
earned by occupying the house rather than by ruling it. A tenth-house Sun is strong
where it stands, regardless of what the tenth lord is doing elsewhere.

In the diurnal sense this is a midday birth — the Sun at its highest point, which is
the geometry the symbolism is drawn from rather than a coincidence alongside it.

## What the tradition actually claims

Vocation in which the native's name is attached to the decision. Government,
institutional or public-facing work. Reputation that accumulates rather than arrives,
and authority the native is comfortable being held responsible for. The sources are
unusually unanimous, and they are describing a disposition towards visible work, not an
office attained.

## The caution

Popular readings turn this placement into a guarantee of high position. It is not one.
About one chart in twelve carries it, and most of those lives are ordinary working
lives in which being credited matters more than it does to other people.

The second caution is the one the tenth-house entry raises about the fourth: standing
of this kind is traditionally read as something the native keeps paying for, in a
currency the tenth house does not itself show.
