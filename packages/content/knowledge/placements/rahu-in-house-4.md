The fourth house is read by what settles. Rahu does not settle. Of the twelve this is
among the placements the classical sources treat least favourably, and the reason is
structural rather than moral: appetite lands in the one house whose signification is
the absence of appetite.

## What makes it difficult

Directional strength in the fourth belongs to the Moon and Venus — the two
significators of comfort — and Rahu is conventionally inimical to the Moon. Nothing in
the dignity or dig bala schemes supports a node here. The practical reading is
restlessness in the private base: a home that is made rather than received, frequently
at a distance from where the native began.

## The relocation reading

The fourth's property signification combined with Rahu's foreignness is traditionally
read as a dwelling away from the native's origin — relocation, land held elsewhere, a
domestic arrangement organised unlike the family's. This is the most practically useful
thing the placement says, and it complements the twelfth rather than competing with it.

## The axis is the chart's architecture

Ketu occupies the tenth by construction. The fourth-tenth tension the house document
describes is therefore not an incidental risk here but the shape of the configuration —
and it runs the less familiar way round: private life demanding attention while public
standing is held cheaply. Saying it in those terms is more accurate than the usual
version.

## Handling the mother

Fourth-house material about the mother already needs room to be wrong. With Rahu here
the pull toward a verdict — distance, difficulty, a specific history — is strong, and
it should be refused. The placement is read as a foundation that was not
straightforward. What that actually was is something only the native can supply.
