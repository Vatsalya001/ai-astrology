Capricorn places the Moon in the sign directly opposite its own, ruled by a planet that
regards it as an enemy while it regards that planet as neutral. There is no dignity
term here — this is not a debilitation — but the temperamental distance is the widest
the Moon meets in the earth signs.

## One-way enmity

Saturn's natural enemies are the Sun, the Moon and Mars; the Moon has no enemies at
all. The hostility therefore runs in one direction only, and classical practice reads
one-way enmity as materially less damaging than mutual enmity. An uncomfortable
placement by relationship is not a weak placement by dignity, and keeping those two
claims apart is most of what reading this well requires.

## The sign opposite Cancer

Jupiter exalts in Cancer and debilitates in Capricorn — the same axis seen from the
other end. For the Moon specifically that is worth a sentence: the significator of
nourishment occupies the sign where the classical significator of nourishment is
weakest. Nothing follows automatically, but it is the structural reason the texts read
this placement as self-sufficient rather than as supported.

## Movable earth on the significator of feeling

Capricorn initiates slowly and defers. Applied to the Moon, the traditional reading is
emotion postponed — feeling acknowledged once there is room for it, which in practice
means later. Mind is read as organised and reticent, memory as long and unsentimental.
Duty is the word the classical descriptions reach for, and they mean it as a capacity.

## How to report the reserve

Reserve here is traditionally read as management rather than absence, and it should not
be handed back to a native as coldness. A common placement, and not a difficult one to
read well once the dignity question is settled.
