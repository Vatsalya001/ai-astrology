The Sun holds directional strength in the tenth. The fourth is the point opposite it,
which makes this the one house where the Sun is at its directional weakest — and in the
diurnal sense that is literal: a fourth-house Sun corresponds to a birth near
midnight, with the Sun beneath the earth.

## Two significators in one house

The fourth signifies the mother; the Sun signifies the father. The placement puts both
in the same house, and the traditional reading is a domestic life organised around the
father's position — his work, his standing, his absence, or the household's
arrangement around it. Classical sources also read it as a base that does not settle:
changes of residence, or a home the native is restless inside rather than rested in.

Note what this is not. A kendra is a house of consequence, so this is a strong
placement in the sense of mattering; it is directionally weak in the sense of being far
from where the Sun functions comfortably. Those two statements are both true at once
and should be said together rather than resolved into a verdict.

## Heat in the house of rest

The fourth is where the chart keeps what is not shown to anyone. A planet whose nature
is to be seen is read there as a private life conducted with one eye on how it looks —
the house kept to a standard, the family's reputation felt as the native's own.

## The caution

This is the house most likely to touch material the native has not raised, and the
father is a loaded subject in a way a reading should respect. Tentative framing, room
to be wrong, and no claim about any living person's conduct or health. Directional
weakness describes a planet's position, never a deficiency in the native.
