Cancer is the Moon's sign and the Moon is among the Sun's friends, so the Sun here
occupies a friendly sign — mitra kshetra, a mild positive with no formal dignity
attached. The two luminaries are the one pair whose sympathy runs both ways without
qualification.

## Heat in a watery sign

The Sun is hot and dry by classical temperament; Cancer is cool and moving water. The
combination is read as heat moderated rather than extinguished: authority exercised
through care, position held by being relied upon. Movable modality keeps the placement
from settling, which is what distinguishes it from the Sun in Scorpio, the other watery
sign where the Sun is a friend's guest.

What the texts describe is a sense of self bound up with the people the native is
responsible for. The traditional caution is the matching exposure — a planet of identity
in a sign of receptivity is read as taking the condition of the household personally.

## Jupiter's exaltation, and company

Cancer is Jupiter's exaltation and Mars's debilitation. Neither concerns the Sun
directly, but both change what the Sun is likely to be sharing the sign with. An exalted
Jupiter conjunct the Sun is a strong benefic and a combust one simultaneously, and the
sources argue about which reading dominates. Say which one is being applied.

## Dakshinayana is a transit, not a birth

The Sun's ingress into Cancer marks Karka Sankranti and the start of the southward
course in the classical calendar. That describes the transiting Sun and the ritual year.
A chart with the Sun in Cancer inherits no calendrical quality from the ingress, and
running the two together is a common error rather than a tradition.
