## Technically an enemy sign, thematically a close fit

Venus rules Libra, so by dignity this is an enemy sign — the same mark Taurus carries.
And yet Libra's concern, the claim of one party weighed against another, is the nearest
thing in the zodiac to Jupiter's own significations of law and judgement. The gap
between the dignity and the thematic fit is the substance of this placement. An honest
reading names both and resolves neither; collapsing it either way loses the information.

## The sign's other dignities point the same direction

Libra exalts Saturn, whom Jupiter counts neutral, and debilitates the Sun, whom Jupiter
counts a friend. It is a sign hospitable to constraint and inhospitable to singular
authority. That shapes what Jupiter can do here: equity rather than pronouncement, the
view that holds because it is fair rather than because of who holds it.

## The classical reading

Arbitration, mediation, counsel between parties, law approached as proportion rather
than as precedent. The movable modality gives it reach — Jupiter here is traditionally
read as taking up other people's disputes readily, including ones it was not party to.

## The caution

A faculty that weighs indefinitely concludes nothing, and Jupiter enlarges whatever it
is handed; here it is handed deliberation. Nothing in this placement speaks to the
outcome of any actual dispute — on that, see this corpus's note on the boundary around
legal questions.
