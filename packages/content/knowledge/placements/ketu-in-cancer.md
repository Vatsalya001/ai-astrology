## Disposed by the Moon

Cancer is the Moon's only sign, so the dispositor of this placement is the one planet
whose phase is itself a variable. Ketu has no dignity of its own to appeal to — the
classical table does not include the nodes — which means the whole weight of the reading
falls on the Moon's sign, house, waxing or waning state and affliction. A Ketu in Cancer
with a strong, waxing, well-placed dispositor and one with a weak, waning, afflicted
dispositor are not variations on a single reading.

## When the Moon is in Cancer as well

Node and luminary in the same sign is the eclipse configuration, and in Cancer it occurs
in the Moon's own house of rulership. Classical sources treat a nodal conjunction with
the Moon as among the more consequential things a chart can carry for the mind and the
emotional life. It is read through the Moon, with the rest of the chart consulted, and
never as a claim about anyone's health or about any particular relative.

## Movable water and a significator that does not hold

Cancer receives and keeps, with a boundary around what it keeps. Ketu is traditionally
read as indifference to outcome. The combination is read as a loosened hold on what the
sign exists to protect — security, the domestic base, inherited belonging — which sits
in a reading as equanimity in some lives and as distance in others. The placement does
not distinguish them. Jupiter exalts in Cancer and Mars falls there, which bears on any
co-tenant but not on Ketu.

## What it is not read as

Not a verdict on family, not a statement about a mother, not a prediction about
children, health or conception. Cancer attracts those readings and they exceed what the
configuration supports.
