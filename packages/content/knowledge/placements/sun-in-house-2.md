The second house belongs to none of the four strength classifications — not a kendra,
not a trine, not a dusthana, not an upachaya. That absence is the first thing to say
about this placement: there is no positional rule pushing the reading either way, so a
second-house Sun is read almost entirely through signification and sign.

## Speech that expects to be heeded

The second governs the voice, and the Sun governs position, so the traditional reading
is a manner of speaking that assumes standing — declarative rather than exploratory,
short with contradiction, and remembered. Several classical sources add that the
native is reluctant to retract something said in public, which is the same trait seen
from the other side.

Mercury is often within a few degrees of the Sun, and when the two share this house
the Budha-Aditya material applies directly to speech rather than to intellect in the
abstract. Mercury is also the planet combustion treats most leniently, so the
conjunction is read less destructively here than the word "combust" suggests.

## Resources with a name attached

Classical lists associate the second-house Sun with resources connected to the father
or to a government or institutional source, and with food and the face as secondary
significations. The texts say notably little else — compared with the tenth or the
ninth, the standard commentaries give this placement a line or two. It is an
unremarkable position, and inflating it is worse than admitting that.

## The caution

This is not a wealth indication on its own. The second describes what is retained and
the eleventh what arrives, and no financial reading rests on one planet in one of
them. Nor does the second house's longevity material belong in a reading — see the
second-house entry for why that is a closed door rather than an omission.
