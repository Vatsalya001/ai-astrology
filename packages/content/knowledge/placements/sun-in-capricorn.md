Saturn rules Capricorn and the Sun counts Saturn an enemy, so this is an enemy sign —
shatru kshetra. Neither exaltation nor debilitation applies to the Sun here; the enmity
is the whole of the dignity statement, and it is a moderate verdict rather than a severe
one. Movable earth, under the slowest of the planets.

## The sharpest natural opposition in the scheme

The Sun and Saturn are the most pointed enmity among the seven, and the classical
reasoning is structural rather than mythological: the Sun signifies the person whose
position is acknowledged, Saturn signifies constraint that does not care who you are. A
Sun in Saturn's sign is read as identity that has to be earned against resistance and is
not conceded on account of who the native is.

The texts describe this as slow rather than denied. Movable modality under Saturn makes
Capricorn the most durable of the movable signs, and the Sun here is traditionally read
as authority that accumulates late and holds once it has arrived.

## Uttara Ashadha's first ten degrees

Uttara Ashadha crosses into Capricorn and runs to 10°, and it is the Sun's own nakshatra.
A Sun in the opening stretch of Capricorn is in an enemy's sign and its own nakshatra at
once, in the portion where the nakshatra's endurance is read as becoming institutional.
The two measures pull against each other and both belong in the reading.

## Makara Sankranti belongs to the transit

The Sun's ingress into Capricorn marks the turn to the northward course and is the most
widely observed solar event in the Indian calendar. It concerns the transiting Sun. A
natal Capricorn Sun borrows nothing from it, and importing the festival's auspiciousness
into a birth chart is a category error rather than an interpretation.
