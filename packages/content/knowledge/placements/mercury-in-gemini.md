## Own sign, but not the strongest one

Gemini is swakshetra — Mercury in its own sign, the second dignity after exaltation.
It is worth being precise about what that does and does not include. Mercury's
exaltation is in Virgo, and so is its moolatrikona, the 16 to 20 degree band that
carries most of the practical strength attributed to a planet in its own rashi. Gemini
gives Mercury ownership without either of those, which is exactly why the texts rank
Virgo above it despite both being Mercury's.

## The sign with no other dignity in it

No classical planet exalts or debilitates in Gemini. Mercury's ownership is therefore
the only dignity the sign contains, and in practice the sign is read almost entirely
through Mercury's own condition. That makes this placement unusually self-referential:
there is no competing dignity to weigh it against, and nothing in the sign to moderate
whatever Mercury is doing.

## Dual air, running at its own speed

Mercury in its own dual air sign is traditionally read as fluency without commitment —
the faculty of distinction operating on everything, including itself. Multiple registers
available, several positions held simultaneously and genuinely, explanation offered
faster than it is asked for. Classical descriptions of this placement emphasise verbal
capacity and are notably silent on conclusions.

## Own sign is no protection against company

The caution specific to this placement follows from Mercury's neutrality rather than
from Gemini. Mercury takes the character of what it sits with, and dignity does not
suspend that: an own-sign Mercury conjunct Rahu is still read as persuasion untethered
from truth, and an own-sign Mercury conjunct Saturn is still read as rigour. Checking
the conjunctions before celebrating the dignity is the order that produces a useful
reading.
