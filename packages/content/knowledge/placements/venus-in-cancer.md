## Enemy sign, and an enmity the texts assert more than argue

Cancer belongs to the Moon, and Venus counts the Moon an enemy, so this is an
enemy-sign placement and is read as weakened dignity. The enmity is counterintuitive —
both planets are soft by classification — and the classical justification for it is
thin: the Moon signifies what is taken in, Venus what is wanted, and the texts hold
that feeling and appetite interfere with each other. That is asserted rather than
demonstrated. A reading should carry it as a convention of the scheme, not as something
self-evident.

## Directional strength in the natural fourth

Cancer is the fourth sign of the natural zodiac, and the fourth house is where Venus
holds its directional strength (dig bala). In the natural zodiac, then, Venus's dig
bala falls squarely in its enemy's sign. Where Cancer actually occupies the fourth
house of a chart, this placement carries sign weakness and directional strength at the
same time — a real simultaneous condition, not a contradiction to be resolved in one
direction.

## Movable water on appetite

Cancer contributes receptivity with a boundary, and a movable modality. Venus here is
traditionally read as comfort sought through home and family, attachment expressed as
caretaking, and taste formed by what is familiar rather than by what is admired. The
movable quality adds approach and withdrawal: affection that initiates, then defends
itself.

## What weakened dignity means here

Weakened dignity describes a planet with less force available to act with. It is not a
defect of affection, and nothing in it supports a conclusion about a household, a
parent, or a marriage. The useful question is what else in the chart supplies the
strength this placement does not.
