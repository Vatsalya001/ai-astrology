Pisces is the twelfth sign and Jupiter rules it. The twelfth house is the last dusthana
and the worst-rated of the twelve. A planet's own natural sign in the chart's most
costly house is the sharpest contradiction in this set, and the classical sources split
on it rather than resolving it.

## The split, stated honestly

One tradition reads this placement as moksha: the significator of dharma in the house of
liberation, associated with charity, retreat, and support while living away from home.
Another reads it as vyaya doing exactly what vyaya does — expenditure enlarged,
generosity outrunning the account, loss that is generous rather than small. Both are
classical. Neither is a minority view, and a reading that reports only the first has
chosen the pleasant meaning.

Dignity again belongs to the sign: Jupiter occupies Pisces in the twelfth house only for
an Aries ascendant.

## What Jupiter does not do here

It does not distinguish chosen expenditure from the kind that simply happens. The
twelfth cannot make that distinction on its own, and Jupiter enlarges whichever is
already present. This is the placement where "benefic means amplifying, not improving"
carries the most weight.

## Foreign residence and structured solitude

The practical significations survive the disagreement: residence away from the place of
origin with support in it, a real capacity for solitude, and giving at a scale the
native does not fully account for. Sources associate it with institutions of
retreat — monastic, academic, medical — where the twelfth's isolation is structured
rather than imposed.

## The caution

No spiritual attainment is promised by a placement, and nothing here should be answered
with a purchase. The useful question is where the outflow goes and whether it was
chosen — which the chart can describe and does not decide.
