## The dignity: a friendly sign

Mars rules Aries, and Jupiter counts Mars a friend. This is mitra kshetra — a friendly
sign, with no exaltation and no weakness attached. In the classical ranking
(exaltation, moolatrikona, own sign, friendly, neutral, enemy, debilitation) it sits in
the upper half without being one of the placements the texts single out.

Two smaller marks in Jupiter's favour: Aries exalts the Sun, another of Jupiter's
friends, and it stands fifth from Sagittarius — a trinal relation to Jupiter's own
sign, which the sources note approvingly without building much on it.

## Ignition, not sustained heat

Aries fire begins things. A planet whose whole significance is increase, placed in the
sign least interested in what already exists, is traditionally read as conviction
arriving ahead of its groundwork — the position taken first and justified afterwards.
The movable modality compounds this: expansion that starts well and is handed on.

## What it does to Jupiter's significations

Teaching reads as advocacy rather than instruction. Law reads as reform rather than
precedent. Counsel is given quickly and without having been asked for. The classical
association is with people who found things — schools, movements, practices — rather
than with those who maintain them.

## The caution

Aries is the sign least willing to consult, and Jupiter is the planet of consultation.
That is the real tension here, and it is traditionally read as a teacher who does not
take instruction well. Nothing in the placement indicates a vocation or an outcome; it
describes the register expansion works in.
