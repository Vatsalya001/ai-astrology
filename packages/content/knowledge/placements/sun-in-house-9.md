This is the placement where a significator sits in the house it signifies. The Sun
signifies the father; the ninth is the house of the father. Nothing else in the solar
series raises that problem, and it is the only interesting thing to say about this
combination first.

## Where the texts disagree

Two classical positions apply and they do not reconcile.

The doctrine of karaka bhava nasha holds that a significator placed in its own house
damages what it signifies — the father's affairs unsettled precisely because the
father's planet sits there. The mainstream reading ignores that in this case and treats
the ninth-house Sun as excellent: a trine, the most auspicious house of the twelve, the
significator at home in it.

Commentators split on this and a reading should say so rather than quietly adopt
whichever suits the tone being aimed at. Naming a disagreement is more useful to a
native than a confident average of two incompatible claims.

## Dharma held in the open

Set the doctrinal argument aside and the descriptive reading is consistent across
sources: principle stated publicly, a pull towards teaching, long journeys undertaken
for a reason, and respect for law in its principled rather than procedural sense. The
ninth is what the native has been given, and a Sun here reads as having been given a
position to live up to.

## The caution

Conviction held in public is the hardest kind to revise, and that is the specific risk
in this placement — not dogma as a character flaw, but the cost of having said it
already.

Material about the father deserves tentative framing. The chart indicates a
relationship to accepted authority; it does not report on a living person.
