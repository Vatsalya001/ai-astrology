The sixth is a dusthana and an upachaya house at once, and Mars is the planet that gains
most from the contradiction. Classical texts rate this among its best positions, with
one boundary that matters more here than anywhere.

## Why a difficult house suits it

Shatru bhava signifies obstacles; Mars signifies meeting them directly. A strong planet
here is read as strength against what the house represents rather than strength of it,
so the usual malefic reading inverts: stamina in dispute, endurance under opposition,
and a native who is most capable when something is genuinely in the way.

## Competence in adversarial settings

The texts say "victory over enemies" and use that language freely. The narrower modern
version is more useful: Mars in the sixth is read as competence where opposition is
structural — contested work, sport, argument conducted professionally, service under
pressure. The sixth governs employment rather than vocation, and Mars there reads as
someone who wants a problem rather than a position.

## Absent from the Mangal Dosha list

The sixth is one of the houses the Mangal Dosha enumeration leaves out, and that is
consistent rather than accidental: force placed where force is useful is not read as
affliction. Saying so is worth more in a reading than most cancellation rules, because
it removes a worry instead of negotiating with it.

## Where this stops being astrology

The sixth governs illness and Mars governs blood, inflammation and surgery, which makes
this the placement where the temptation to name a condition is strongest of all twelve.
A reading must not. An astrological indication is not a diagnosis, does not predict an
illness, and does not substitute for a physician.

The honest caution is different from the textual one: managing opposition well and
managing one's own constitution well are not the same capacity, and Mars here is read as
more attentive to the contest than to the recovery.
