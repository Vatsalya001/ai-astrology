This is the combination the classical literature cares most about, and the one where it
most plainly contradicts itself.

## The significator in its own house

Venus is the natural significator of partnership, Libra — which it rules — is the
seventh sign of the natural zodiac, and the seventh is a kendra. Every structural
consideration points at strength.

Against that stands a classical rule, karako bhava nashaya: a significator occupying
the house it signifies is read as damaging it. The rule is most often cited for Jupiter
in the fifth, and the sources extending it to Venus in the seventh are a genuine
minority rather than a fringe. Both positions are traditional, they cannot both be
right, and the literature has not settled it.

## What both camps agree on

Attention substantially organised around partnership: a native for whom relationship is
a primary rather than a secondary concern, in business arrangements as much as in
marriage. The seventh also governs trade and contracts, and Venus there is read as
negotiation conducted agreeably — which the sources treat as an asset and a
vulnerability in the same breath, since agreeableness is what the other party prices.

Where the seventh holds Taurus, Libra or Pisces, the Malavya condition is met in the
house of partnership itself.

## Frequency, against the volume

Venus occupies the seventh in roughly one chart in twelve. The classical descriptions
of this placement are far larger than that frequency can carry. The asymmetry noted in
the house's own document — Venus weighted particularly for men — belongs to the
literature's period rather than to the technique.

## The caution

No verdict about a marriage, in either direction. A strong significator in the house of
partnership is not a statement about whether a relationship forms, holds or ends.
