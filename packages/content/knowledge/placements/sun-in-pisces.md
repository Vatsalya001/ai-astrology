Jupiter rules Pisces and is one of the Sun's friends, so the Sun stands in a friendly
sign — mitra kshetra, with no formal dignity. Dual water under the great benefic, in the
last sign of the zodiac. The dispositor is sympathetic and the medium is not, and that
pairing is the whole character of the placement.

## A friendly sign with a dissolving medium

Pisces is where distinctions stop holding, and the Sun is the planet of distinct
identity. Jupiter's friendly rulership keeps this from reading as an affliction, but the
classical descriptions are consistently about boundaries: identity extending past its own
edges, authority exercised by example rather than by position, and difficulty locating
where the native's own state ends and someone else's begins.

The favourable reading is devotion — the self offered to something larger, which is
Pisces' own vocabulary applied to a solar significator. The unfavourable reading is
dissipation, and the texts treat these as one disposition under different conditions
rather than as two possibilities.

## Mercury's debilitation, and shared tenancy

Pisces debilitates Mercury and exalts Venus, and both planets stay close to the Sun. A
Pisces Sun is therefore often accompanied by a debilitated Mercury, an exalted Venus, or
both — each liable to combustion. The company here is usually more consequential than
the Sun's own condition, which is unusual in this series.

## The gandanta at the end of the sign

The final degrees of Pisces, in Revati, meet the first of Aries at a water–fire junction
the tradition marks as gandanta and reads with care. The same boundary closes the
sidereal solar year. A Sun in the last degrees of Pisces is in a friendly sign at a
junction the texts handle cautiously, and both facts belong in the reading.
