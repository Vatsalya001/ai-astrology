The Sun is a malefic in the classical scheme, and the third is an upachaya house. That
single fact changes the reading: a planet whose heat is a liability elsewhere is read
here as supplying exactly what the house requires.

## Why the classification helps

The third signifies effort mounted against resistance, and a planet that burns is read
as fuel rather than as damage. Classical descriptions of the third-house Sun are
therefore warmer than its reputation elsewhere would suggest — valour, stamina in
short bursts, writing or speaking that carries weight, and journeys undertaken for a
purpose rather than for rest.

The distinctive note is self-authorisation. The third is initiative taken without
permission, and the Sun is the planet that expects to be the one deciding; the
combination is traditionally read as a native who begins before a mandate arrives.

## The sibling claim, and why it is not delivered

Several classical sources read a malefic in the third as difficulty for younger
siblings, and the Sun appears in those lists. A reading does not pass that on. It is a
statement about a third party who is not present, cannot consent to it, and whose
circumstances the chart in front of us says nothing verifiable about. Naming the
tradition is honest; repeating its conclusion as a finding is not.

## The caution

The heat here sits in the house of hands, writing and communication, so the
traditional risk is announcement outrunning completion — the project described before
it exists, the position stated before it is held. That is a working habit worth
noticing, and it is the useful thing this placement has to say.
