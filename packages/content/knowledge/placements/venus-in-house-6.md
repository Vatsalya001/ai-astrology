Three separate rules point the same way here, which makes this the most pointed of the
twelve for Venus.

## Three readings that stack

The sixth is a dusthana. It is also an upachaya house, where malefics strengthen and a
benefic is read less favourably — the house's own document makes that point with
Jupiter. And Virgo is the sixth sign of the natural zodiac, which is Venus's
debilitation: of all twelve houses, this is the one whose natural sign is the planet's
weakest.

The convergence is worth naming because it is unusual. For most planets these three
considerations pull in different directions and have to be weighed against each other.

## Appetite as an obligation

The sixth governs service and employment as distinct from vocation, daily discipline,
and debt. Venus here is read as what the native wants becoming something they owe —
pleasure organised into routine, or comfort acquired on terms. Several sources note
that Venus in difficult houses produces agreeable-looking versions of those
difficulties, and they name the sixth specifically.

The constructive reading is work. The sixth is employment, and the aesthetic and
relational trades sit here naturally — hospitality, grooming, care, anything where the
service itself is the product.

## Two incompatible classical positions

Some texts place this among Venus's weakest positions, on the debilitation resonance.
Others apply the dusthana-strength logic and read it as durability against opposition
in the arena Venus governs. The disagreement is genuine and unresolved; a reading
should state which convention it is using rather than blend them.

## The caution

Nothing diagnostic, ever — the medical boundary is absolute. And this is the placement
most often converted into a judgement about a native's appetites, which the material
does not support in either direction.
