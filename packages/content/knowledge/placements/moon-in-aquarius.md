Aquarius is Saturn's other sign and carries the same one-way enmity, so the question
worth asking is not whether this differs from Capricorn by dignity — it does not — but
what the same lord's discipline is being applied to.

## The same lord, a different object

In Capricorn Saturn's restraint operates on circumstances; in Aquarius it operates on
ideas. For the Moon that difference is the whole placement: the restraint lands on the
feeling itself rather than on the schedule for having it. Aquarius carries no
exaltation or debilitation for any of the seven, so there is no dignity term pulling in
either direction.

## No dignity penalty, the largest mismatch of temperament

The Moon is the most personal body in the scheme and Aquarius the most impersonal sign
in it. That pairing is read as the sharpest temperamental mismatch available to the Moon
while carrying no formal penalty whatsoever — which makes it the clearest case in the
set for why dignity and temperament are separate axes that should be reported
separately. Classical sources are reserved about it and specific about the reservation:
detachment, not weakness.

## Fixed air and the collective

The reading is emotional attention given to the category rather than to the instance —
concern for people in general, held consistently, with the particular person somewhat
outside the frame. Fixed modality makes the position durable: a settled view about what
ought to be felt is traditionally read as harder to shift here than the feeling it
replaced.

## Where the detachment becomes a cost

Principle standing in for warmth, and reluctance to admit a need that cannot be
justified in general terms. A common placement, and one a reading should describe
carefully rather than diagnose.
