Mars in Taurus is one of the combinations the classical texts have least to say about.
That is useful information rather than a gap to be filled.

## Neutral dignity

Taurus belongs to Venus, who is neither Mars's friend nor its enemy, so Mars here sits
in a neutral sign — no dignity, no disability, nothing added or subtracted by the
rulership. Taurus is also the Moon's exaltation, and the Moon is Mars's friend, so Mars
does not trouble the sign's most consequential dignity the way an enemy of the Moon
would.

## The whole of the story is modality

Mars is the quickest-acting of the classical planets and Taurus is fixed earth. A
movable temperament in a sign that sustains is the only real tension in this placement,
and it resolves in Taurus's favour: the force slows down and keeps going. Effort becomes
steady and hard to redirect. Initiative is the casualty; endurance is the gain.

## Where it reads well

Mars signifies land and property; Taurus signifies accumulation and the tangible. The
two sets of significations meet without friction, which is why the sources that do
comment on this placement comment on property, physical craft, and work with materials.
Patient, hands-on, result-bearing effort is the defensible reading.

## The caution

Force held too long in one direction. The standard warning about fixed signs — that the
situation changed and the planet did not — applies to Mars more sharply than to most,
because Mars's method is pressure. Anger here is traditionally read as slow to arrive
and slow to leave, which is a temperamental note and not a claim about anyone's conduct.
