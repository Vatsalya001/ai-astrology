## The only enemy sign Mercury has

Mercury declares exactly one enemy in the natural-friendship table: the Moon. The Moon
rules exactly one sign. So Cancer is the single enemy sign Mercury can occupy anywhere
in the zodiac, which makes this placement structurally distinctive even though it is no
rarer than any other — roughly one chart in twelve.

The enmity runs one way. The Moon counts Mercury a friend, so a Moon-Mercury conjunction
in Cancer is read as supportive for the Moon and weakening for Mercury at the same time.
That is a real asymmetric condition rather than a contradiction to be resolved, and
both halves belong in a reading.

## Movable water on the sorting faculty

Mercury's work is distinguishing one thing from another. Cancer is water with a shell:
receptive, retentive, and defended. The placement is traditionally read as a mind that
reasons from memory and sorts by feeling — vivid recall of episodes, accurate about who
said what and how it landed, and reluctant to separate what happened from how it felt.
Enemy sign weakens the discriminating function specifically, not the intelligence.

The caution follows directly: an argument in this register can be a grievance with
structure added afterwards. The traditional advice is to check whether the conclusion
preceded the reasoning, which is a question about the placement and not about the person.

## Strong Jupiter, weak Mars, weak Mercury

Cancer is Jupiter's exaltation and Mars's debilitation, so a Mercury here can share the
sign with the greatest benefic at full strength. Mercury takes the character of its
companions, and with Jupiter that character is wisdom — a weakened Mercury next to an
exalted Jupiter is traditionally read as judgement that is sound without the native
being able to show the working.

Enemy sign is a dignity, not a verdict. It describes a faculty operating in unfamiliar
terrain.
