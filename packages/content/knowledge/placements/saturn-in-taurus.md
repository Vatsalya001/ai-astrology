A friendly placement that the classical texts pass over quickly — which is itself
information. Nothing here needs managing.

## The dignity

Friendly sign. Venus rules Taurus and is one of Saturn's two friends, the other being
Mercury. No classical planet is debilitated in Taurus and the Moon is exalted there, so
Saturn sits in a sign with a reputation for hosting well rather than in one that has to
be worked around.

## The closest terrain to its own

Earth is the element of Saturn's Capricorn, and fixed is the modality of sustaining. Of
the signs Saturn neither rules nor is exalted in, this is the nearest thing to home:
accumulation over time is the sign's method and the planet's both. The significations
align instead of negotiating, which is why there is little classical commentary — there
is no tension to resolve.

## What it is read as doing

Labour is traditionally read as productive and unhurried, and structure as applied to
tangible things: craft, property, bodily routine, the significations of resources held
rather than circulated. Persistence is read as native rather than summoned.

## The risk here is not weakness

It is immobility. Saturn is already the slowest of the classical seven, and a fixed sign
removes the last argument for changing direction. A planet that is reliable and
difficult to redirect is an asset while the situation holds and a liability once it has
changed. That is the specific caution for Saturn in Taurus, and it is the exact inverse
of the caution attached to Aries.

## A functional note

Saturn rules the ninth and tenth from a Taurus ascendant, making it a functional benefic
for those charts — a natural malefic doing favourable structural work. Worth checking
before the word "malefic" is allowed to do any work in the reading.
