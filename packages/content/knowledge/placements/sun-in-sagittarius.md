Jupiter rules Sagittarius and counts among the Sun's friends, so the Sun occupies a
friendly sign with no formal dignity — mitra kshetra. Dual fire under the natural great
benefic is the most hospitable arrangement the Sun gets outside Aries and Leo, and the
sources read it mildly favourably without making strong claims for it.

## Fire with a benefic dispositor

Fire reinforcing fire would ordinarily raise the question of excess. Jupiter's rulership
is what moderates it: the traditional reading is identity in service of a principle
rather than of itself — authority derived from a doctrine, a body of law, or a subject
taught. Where the Sun in Aries acts from its own nature, the Sun in Sagittarius acts from
a position it can justify.

Dual modality lets the aim change without the character changing, which is the quality
the texts say Sagittarius has and the other fire signs lack. For a planet of identity
that reads as a native who can revise the target and remain recognisably themselves.

## Uttara Ashadha's fiery portion

From 26°40' of Sagittarius the Sun stands in Uttara Ashadha, its own nakshatra, whose
shakti is durable victory. A late-Sagittarius Sun holds nakshatra dignity on top of a
friendly sign.

## A calendrical note that is not a natal reading

The transiting Sun's month in Sagittarius is Dhanurmasa, which several regional
traditions treat as unsuitable for scheduling auspicious undertakings. That is a muhurta
convention about a transit. It says nothing about someone born with the Sun here, and
conflating the two is common and avoidable.

## The shortest of the twelve months

The Sun moves fastest through this stretch of the sidereal zodiac, spending about two
days less here than in Gemini — marginally fewer than one chart in twelve.
