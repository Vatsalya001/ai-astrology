Mars in Pisces is a friendly sign that nonetheless reads weakly, and holding those two
facts together is the whole difficulty of the placement.

## Friendly dignity, unhelpful terrain

Jupiter rules Pisces and is Mars's friend, so the formal dignity is friendly —
supported. What the dignity does not describe is the medium. Dual water under Jupiter
gives Mars no edge to work with: the friendship is what keeps the texts from reading
this badly, while the element and the modality are what keep them from reading it
strongly.

## Not the same as Cancer

Both are water signs, and the comparison is the useful one. Cancer is Mars's
debilitation under a movable modality, and force there is read as turning inward with
nowhere to go. Pisces is friendly and dual, and force there is read as going outward and
dispersing. The difference is not the element; it is the rulership, and what the
modality does with the energy.

## What is traditionally read

Effort on behalf of others, discharged unevenly. Energy that arrives with sympathy and
departs when the sympathy does. The reading favours Mars's service significations over
its competitive ones — care, rescue, devotional discipline, anything whose motive is
another person's state rather than a contest. Physical energy is described as
intermittent rather than as low.

## The caution

Force with no boundary. The traditional notes are motivation that depends on mood,
frustration that cannot name its object, and effort given away faster than it is
replaced. Pisces is where distinctions stop holding, and Mars's method requires a
distinction — something to push against. Reading this placement as laziness is a
misreading: the sources describe energy that is hard to aim, not energy that is absent.
