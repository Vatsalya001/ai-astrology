## Exaltation — and why 27° matters

Pisces is Venus's exaltation (uccha), the strongest dignity available in the classical
scheme, and it is exact at 27° Pisces. Strength is normally graded by proximity to that
degree, so a Venus at 4° Pisces is exalted by sign while holding a good deal less of
what the exaltation is read to confer. The label and the quantity are two different
claims, and only one of them survives looking at the degree.

## The dispositor counts Venus an enemy

Here is the inversion that makes this placement structurally interesting. Pisces
belongs to Jupiter, which Venus counts neutral and which counts Venus an enemy. Venus
therefore reaches its maximum dignity in a sign owned by a planet unfriendly to it, and
its minimum in a sign owned by a friend. Mercury is debilitated in Pisces as well, so
Venus's strongest sign is also where that friend is weakest. Sign dignity and natural
friendship are separate axes, and Venus demonstrates their independence more cleanly
than any other planet does.

## What an exalted benefic amplifies

Exaltation is maximum capacity, not guaranteed benefit. The classical account of
Venus's benefic nature is amplifying rather than protective — see the planet's own
document on what it does to things that do not deserve improving — and exaltation runs
that mechanism at full strength. The practical consequence is counterintuitive: the
house matters more here, not less. An exalted Venus in a difficult house is read as
that difficulty with better presentation.

## Malavya, and the limit of the claim

Pisces is the only sign Venus does not rule that still satisfies the Malavya condition,
when the placement falls in a kendra. That yoga's caveats apply in full, including that
Venus's sign is partly a function of the time of year. Strength available is not an
outcome promised.
