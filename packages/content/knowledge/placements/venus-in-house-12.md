Pisces is the twelfth sign of the natural zodiac and Venus is exalted in it. The
strongest dignity this planet can hold falls, in the natural zodiac, inside a dusthana
— and the mirror holds too: its debilitation sign is the natural sixth. Venus is the
one planet whose best and worst signs both land in difficult houses.

## Exaltation in a house of expenditure

Pisces's own document reads Venus's exaltation there as appetite without object, which
the texts treat as either devotion or dissipation depending on the rest of the chart.
The twelfth house supplies the same fork in different vocabulary: it governs what is
let go, and on its own it does not distinguish what was given from what was merely
lost. Venus in the twelfth is that ambiguity at full strength — attachment spent, with
the rest of the chart deciding on what.

## Where the sources genuinely disagree

The twelfth is a dusthana, and yet a number of classical texts read Venus there
favourably on one specific count: the house's association with private comfort and the
pleasures of rest. That is among the clearer internal contradictions in the literature
— the same placement called weak by classification and good by signification. Both
readings are traditional. Neither cancels the other, and a reading should carry both.

## Solitude and distance

The twelfth governs isolation, charity and foreign residence. Venus here is read as
what the native does without company — private aesthetic practice, work made for no
audience — and as relationships or occupation located away from the place of origin.

## The caution

This is the house where the classical reading and the popular one diverge most widely,
and an exalted Venus is the easiest place to pick the pleasant meaning. The sources
treat the twelfth as costly and read the valuable version as what becomes available
once the cost is accepted, not as a substitute for it.
