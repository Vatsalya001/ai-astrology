## The dignity

Neutral sign. Jupiter rules Pisces and is Saturn's neutral, so formally this sits in the
middle grade: no strength, no weakness. Venus is exalted in Pisces and Mercury
debilitated; Saturn is neither.

## Why the dignity table is the wrong instrument here

Sagittarius is Jupiter's other sign and is neutral in exactly the same way, and it reads
as genuinely unremarkable. Pisces does not, and the reason is worth being precise about:
dignity measures strength, while the interesting thing here is a conflict of
significations. Saturn's whole contribution is the boundary — limit, definition, the line
past which something is not permitted. Pisces is the sign in which distinctions stop
holding. A planet at middling strength can still be doing something the terrain will not
take.

## What it is read as doing

Structure that will not keep its edges. Classical sources run in two directions and both
are worth reporting: discipline turned towards something without form — the significations
of renunciation, long solitary practice, service with no audience, the twelfth-house
register this sign carries in the natural zodiac — or discipline that dissolves, where
the rule is made and then quietly not applied. Which of the two a chart supports is a
question for the house and the aspects. The sign alone does not settle it.

## Dual water, and a timing note

Pisces is dual-bodied, and dual signs are the register in which transitions happen.
Saturn is the slowest body in the classical set. The combination is read as change that
takes a long while to become visible and is then already finished — which is why Saturn's
passage through this sign tends to get described afterwards rather than during.

## The caution

Nothing here authorises a claim about loss. The twelfth-house vocabulary that attaches to
Pisces concerns release and expenditure, and a sign placement does not convert that into
an event.
