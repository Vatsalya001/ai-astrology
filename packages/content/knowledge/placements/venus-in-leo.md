## The one mutual enmity

Leo is the Sun's only sign, and Venus and the Sun count each other enemies — the
enmity is recorded from both sides, which is uncommon in a scheme whose friendships are
frequently one-directional. Venus in Leo is an enemy-sign placement, and after the
Virgo debilitation it is the most squarely weakened of the twelve.

## Combustion is a live question here

Venus's bounded elongation from the Sun has a specific consequence for this sign. When
Venus occupies Leo, the Sun is always within two signs of it and is often in Leo
itself, so an enemy sign and a close Sun arrive together far more often than chance
would arrange — and the sources treat the pair as compounding rather than independent.
Leo also holds no exaltation or debilitation among the classical seven, so the sign is
read largely through the Sun's own condition. This placement therefore depends on the
Sun's state more than most Venus placements depend on anything.

## Fixed fire on a planet that often works privately

Leo contributes sustained visibility; a planet here is read as unwilling to operate
anonymously. That cuts against Venus in particular, because much of what Venus
signifies — private comfort, aesthetic practice, intimate attachment — ordinarily
works with no audience at all. The traditional reading is affection that wants
witnessing, art made to be seen, generosity performed rather than concealed; and the
liability the texts name is the cost when the witnessing does not arrive.

## What the sources do not say

They do not say this placement makes someone vain, and they do not say it damages a
relationship. An enemy sign plus possible combustion is a statement about available
strength. It fixes no outcome, and none should be offered.
