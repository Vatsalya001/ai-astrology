The Moon in the twelfth house is the one placement in this set where the planet's
signification and the house's are the same thing. The twelfth governs sleep and dreams;
the Moon governs the mind. No other pairing in the chart has that overlap.

## Dreams and the interior

Classical sources read a twelfth-house Moon as an unusually active inner life: vivid
dreaming, a mind that keeps working on things while the native is not watching, and
emotional processing that happens privately and is never reported. The useful version
of this is that feeling is not available for inspection — including by the native.

The twelfth is a dusthana, and the Moon is exposed here in the plain sense: emotional
attention directed at what is lost, given away, or kept out of sight.

## Solitude and distance

Foreign residence is the twelfth's most practically useful signification, and with the
Moon it is read as emotional distance from the place of origin — relocation that
separates, and comfort found in that separation at least as often as difficulty.

Isolation is the darker reading, and the sources supply both. Capacity for solitude and
inability to be accompanied look identical from outside and are not the same condition.

## The caution runs in both directions

The twelfth is where the distance between the classical reading and the popular one is
widest, and a Moon in it attracts the worst of each. One direction pathologises the
placement into a lonely, sorrowful mind. The other romanticises it into a spiritual
nature with liberation close at hand. Classical sources treat the twelfth as costly
first, and treat the spiritual reading as what becomes available once the cost has been
accepted.

Neither version is a finding. The defensible statement is that emotional life is
conducted privately, which a great many people experience as entirely workable.
