The first house is the only one that is both a kendra and a trine, which is the
strongest pair of classifications a house carries. A Sun here is traditionally read as
a self that is not held in reserve.

## Exaltation is a property of the sign, not the house number

The natural zodiac puts Aries on the first, and Aries is the Sun's exaltation — which
is why this placement is so often described as exalted in general terms. In an actual
chart that holds only for an Aries ascendant. For a Libra ascendant the same house
number is the Sun's debilitation. The house establishes the arena; the sign
establishes the dignity, and conflating the two is the most common error in reading
this combination.

## What the texts emphasise

Classical descriptions are consistent and fairly blunt: a presence strangers register
immediately, impatience with being managed, and a reluctance to occupy a role without
being named in it. The standard lists also attach the head and the eyes to a
first-house Sun. That is a traditional association between a significator and a part
of the body, not an observation about health, and it belongs nowhere near a
diagnosis.

A birth near sunrise puts the Sun at or just past the ascendant, so this placement and
a dawn birth time tend to appear together.

## The caution

This is the solar placement most inflated by popular readings, usually into the word
"leader." About one chart in twelve has a first-house Sun — it cannot carry that
weight. The honest traditional reading is narrower and more useful: a native for whom
being unacknowledged is the specific difficulty, which is a tendency to describe, not
a rank to award.
