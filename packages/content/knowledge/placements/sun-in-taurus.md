The Sun in Taurus sits in an enemy's sign. Venus rules Taurus and the Sun counts Venus
an enemy — that is the entire dignity statement available here, since Taurus grants the
Sun neither exaltation nor debilitation. Fixed earth under a Venusian dispositor is the
slowest medium the Sun occupies.

## A dignity the texts decline to dramatise

Enemy-sign placement, shatru kshetra, is a moderate verdict several steps milder than
debilitation, and the classical sources treat it that way. The reading is identity that
needs something tangible to rest on: position confirmed by what has been accumulated
rather than by what has been declared. Fixed modality adds duration, so the Sun here is
read as holding a place rather than taking one.

The enmity itself is informative rather than hostile. Venus signifies enjoyment taken on
its own terms, and a planet whose nature is to be central is read as poorly served by a
sign that measures worth in comfort.

## Krittika, and why early Taurus differs

Krittika crosses the Aries–Taurus boundary and runs to 10° of Taurus, and Krittika is
the Sun's own nakshatra. A Sun in the first ten degrees of Taurus is therefore in an
enemy's sign and its own nakshatra at the same time. The two measures genuinely disagree,
and a reading that reports only one of them is incomplete. In the Taurus portion
Krittika is traditionally read as refinement rather than ignition.

## The luminaries part company

Taurus is the Moon's exaltation. The sign that gives the emotional nature its best ground
gives the solar nature a mildly unfriendly one — worth noticing in any chart where both
luminaries are here, since a new moon in Taurus holds a strong Moon and an unremarkably
placed Sun in the same degrees.
