The Sun is exalted in Aries — uccha, the strongest dignity the scheme gives it, with the
deep point of exaltation at 10° of the sign. Mars rules Aries and counts among the Sun's
friends, so dispositor and occupant are in sympathy. Movable fire under a friendly fiery
dispositor is the most concentrated arrangement available to this planet.

## What exaltation is a claim about

Exaltation is read as a planet able to act fully in its own character, not as a planet
that is fortunate. For the Sun that means identity asserted without reference to
precedent: the native as the first authority in a situation rather than the next holder
of one. Classical descriptions run to self-sufficiency, physical vigour, and a
reluctance to wait for permission.

The traditional caution follows from the same heat. An exalted Sun is still a malefic by
nature, and whatever shares the sign with it is read as burned rather than warmed. Aries
can hold a very strong Sun and a badly combust companion at once.

## The sign's own arrangement

Aries carries the Sun's exaltation and Saturn's debilitation together, which is the
clearest statement the dignity table makes about these two: the sign that gives
self-assertion its best ground gives structure its worst. Where both planets occupy
Aries, a reading has to hold the two conclusions separately rather than average them.

The last three degrees and twenty minutes of Aries fall in Krittika, the Sun's own
nakshatra, so a late-Aries Sun carries strength on two independent measures.

## Frequency

The Sun spends about a month here, so roughly one chart in twelve carries an exalted
Sun. It is neither rare nor a mark of distinction. Exaltation describes a condition of
the planet, never an outcome in a life.
