Libra is the Sun's debilitation — neecha, the weakest dignity in the scheme, with the
deep point at 10° of the sign. Venus rules Libra and the Sun counts Venus an enemy, so
occupant and dispositor are unsympathetic as well. Movable air under Venus is the least
solar medium there is.

## What debilitation is a statement about

It is a statement about the planet's capacity to act in its own character. It is not a
statement about the native's worth or ability. The classical reasoning is specific:
Libra's business is proportion between parties, and a planet whose nature is to be
central cannot do that work without surrendering the thing that makes it itself.
Descriptions run to identity held provisionally, authority exercised by consent, and
recognition arriving through other people rather than directly.

Read favourably — and the tradition does, in places — this is the Sun obliged to become
diplomatic. The capacity is real; the texts simply note that it is purchased.

## Neecha bhanga, and why to be strict

Libra is Saturn's exaltation, and several of the standard cancellation conditions turn on
where Saturn and Venus sit. That makes this debilitation among the easiest in the whole
scheme to argue away, which is exactly why it warrants discipline: name the specific
condition that applies, or do not claim the cancellation at all.

## Frequency first

The Sun spends about a month in Libra, so roughly one person in twelve has a debilitated
Sun. A description that reads like a misfortune is describing eight percent of everyone,
and is therefore describing nothing in particular.

## What a reading may not do with this

No claim about physical vitality in the medical sense, about a father's circumstances, or
about standing being withheld. The placement describes a manner, not an outcome.
