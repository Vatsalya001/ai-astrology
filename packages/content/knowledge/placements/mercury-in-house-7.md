## The contract placement

The seventh governs partnership, contracts and trade; Mercury governs commerce,
calculation and the language an agreement is written in. Of the twelve houses this is
where Mercury's commercial signification lands most literally, and the classical emphasis
falls on business partnership specifically — dealings that are negotiated, stated and
documented rather than assumed. Marriage is the seventh's better-known signification and
the one where this placement says least.

## What the native brings

The house rule is that occupants of the seventh describe what the native brings to a
partnership rather than what they receive. Mercury brings articulation: stating a
position, hearing another, finding terms between them. Libra, the seventh's natural sign,
is Venus-ruled and Venus is Mercury's friend in the classical scheme, so where that
correspondence holds the sign reinforces rather than complicates the placement.

## Angular, but not directionally strong

The seventh is a kendra, which lends weight to any occupant. Directional strength here
belongs to Saturn — Mercury's dig bala sits in the first, the opposite house. So a
Mercury in the seventh is strong by angularity and neutral by direction. Distinguishing
those two is worth the sentence, because collapsing them into "strong" is how a
first-house reading gets applied to a seventh-house chart.

## The caution

A partner who can always explain their position is not the same as a partner who is
right, and Mercury is read as supplying the first. On marriage the house document's limit
is absolute and nothing here softens it: a reading names tensions and strengths, and
never a verdict about a person or a match. Seventh-house afflictions are also far more
common than their reputation suggests, with extensive classical cancellations — check
frequency before weighting anything found here.
