Mars in the first house is the only position where its aspect doctrine becomes literal,
and that single fact explains most of the placement's reputation.

## The aspect set becomes literal

Mars aspects the 4th, 7th and 8th from wherever it sits. From the first house those are
the fourth, seventh and eighth houses themselves — domestic peace, partnership and
discontinuity. The three areas classical texts consider least suited to force are the
exact three it lands on. This is one rationale for the first house appearing on the
Mangal Dosha list, and it is more defensible than the list taken as bare enumeration.

## Dignity, and where it applies

The first house holds Aries in the natural zodiac, Mars's own sign. That correspondence
is literal only for an Aries ascendant, and by the second rulership for a Scorpio
ascendant. For the other ten, Mars here sits in someone else's sign and the reading
softens accordingly. Dignity is read from the rising sign, never inferred from the
house number.

## What is traditionally read

The first house is read as the traits strangers notice, and Mars there is traditionally
read as directness — physical restlessness, a short route to the point, and a
willingness to be disliked early rather than misunderstood late. The temperamental
signature is acting before the deliberation has finished.

Classical sources also record a scar or distinguishing mark about the head or face.
That appears in the texts as an identifying detail, in the way a physical description
would be given, not as an event foretold.

## The caution

Wounds appear in the classical lists for this placement and that material is
descriptive, not prognostic. Reading it as a forecast of physical harm is unsupported
and does real damage. What the first house defensibly describes is constitution and
manner — a presence that costs something to maintain — and that is not a statement
about worth.
