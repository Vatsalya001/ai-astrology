## A friend's sign, with a caveat about the friend

Gemini belongs to Mercury, and Venus counts Mercury a friend — so this is a
friendly-sign placement: supported, but short of own-sign or exaltation. The caveat is
unusual. Mercury has no fixed character of its own to lend, taking the colour of
whatever it sits with, so the support a friendly dispositor would normally imply depends
here on Mercury's company rather than on Mercury itself. Checking what Mercury is
conjunct is how you find out how friendly this sign actually is.

## The quietest sign in the dignity scheme

Gemini holds no exaltation and no debilitation among the classical seven — the only
air sign of which that is true alongside Aquarius. For Venus that compounds into a
genuinely unremarkable placement: a friendly sign with no dignity landmarks in it at
all. Saying so is more useful than manufacturing significance for it.

## Dual air: appetite that explains itself

What the sign contributes is plurality and articulacy. Venus here is traditionally read
as attraction mediated by language — charm that works through speech, preference that
can be argued for, art made of words rather than of materials. The dual modality is
read as holding more than one thing at once, which the sign's own literature insists is
plurality and not indecision.

## The verdict the texts decline to give

Modern writing often turns this placement into unreliability in affection. The
classical sources are reserved about commitment in dual signs; they do not deliver that
verdict, and the slide from "provisional" to "faithless" was added later. What is
defensible is that this is traditionally read as keeping options described rather than
closed — a disposition, not a prediction about anyone.
