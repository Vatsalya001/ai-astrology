The second house gives its occupants a direct effect on speech, and Ketu's is the most
particular of the set. Where Rahu here is read as persuasion outrunning accuracy, Ketu
is read as the opposite error: speech that says less than the native knows. Elliptical,
clipped, withholding — not from evasion, but because the impulse to elaborate is absent.

## Resources held loosely

Dhana bhava governs what is kept rather than what arrives. Ketu here is traditionally
read as weak attachment to accumulation: possessions that are not defended, savings not
tracked, an inheritance received without much interest in it. Classical texts are
matter-of-fact about this and do not treat it as poverty — the eleventh governs income,
and Ketu in the second says nothing about how much comes in.

No reading may state that a native will or will not become wealthy. What is available
here is structural: whether resources consolidate, and how much the native cares whether
they do.

## Where the classical material stops

The second is one of the houses texts associate with longevity questions, and Ketu is
the planet those texts associate with loss. The combination is the one most likely to
invite a statement about death, and the literature does contain methods for producing
one. They are deliberately not developed in this corpus and must not be reconstructed
from it. The methods disagree with each other, and no version of that answer is
responsible to give.

## A caution about family

Second-house material reaches family and food as well as money. A native with this
placement may say the family's resources simply never interested them, which is a report
about attention and not a judgement on the family.
