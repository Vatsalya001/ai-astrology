The fourth is a kendra, and its natural sign is Cancer — Jupiter's exaltation. That
coincidence is why the classical sources describe this placement more warmly than most,
and the reason needs stating carefully before the warmth is used.

## Sign and house are not the same claim

Dignity belongs to signs, not houses. Jupiter is exalted in the fourth house only when
Cancer actually occupies it — which is to say, for an Aries ascendant. The rest of the
enthusiasm is about the natural zodiac's resonance: the house of the mother holding the
sign of the Moon, with the great benefic exalted in it. Symbolically strong,
technically separate. A reading that calls every Jupiter in the fourth exalted has
merged the two.

Because the fourth is a kendra, Jupiter in Cancer, Sagittarius or Pisces here does form
Hamsa yoga.

## What it is read as

An enlarged base: a settled home, support from the mother, land. Jupiter and the Moon
are natural friends, so the placement reads cleanly where the Moon is also well
disposed. Classical associations are inherited comfort and a house that other people
are fed in.

## It softens the chart's standard tension

From the fourth, Jupiter aspects the eighth, tenth and twelfth. The tenth is the one
that matters: the pattern of a public life bought with a private one is the most
commonly named tension in a chart, and this is one of the few configurations
traditionally read as supplying standing *from* the private base rather than at its
expense.

## The caution

Comfort and satisfaction are not the same reading, and the sources split on which this
is — security, or a reason never to leave. Nothing in the placement indicates an
acquisition: it describes a relationship to home and land, not the obtaining of either.
