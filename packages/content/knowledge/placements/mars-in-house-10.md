This is the strongest position Mars has, and unusually the strength axes do not
contradict each other.

## Four axes pointing the same way

The tenth is a kendra and an upachaya house, so a malefic there is strengthened twice
over by house class alone. Mars holds directional strength in the tenth — dig bala,
which it shares with the Sun. And the tenth holds Capricorn in the natural zodiac,
Mars's sign of exaltation. Angularity, house class, direction and dignity all agree,
which almost never happens for any planet, and is why the classical texts are close to
unanimous here.

## How often all four actually hold

Only the first three come free with the house. Exaltation needs Capricorn in the tenth,
which means an Aries ascendant — so Mars in the tenth occurs in roughly one chart in
twelve, and Mars exalted there in well under one per cent. Mars in own sign or exalted
while in a kendra is the condition named Ruchaka, which has its own entry; this document
is about the placement, not the yoga.

## What is read

Karma bhava is action that can be seen, and Mars there is traditionally read as a
working life organised around visible effort: command, enforcement, construction,
engineering, defence, surgery, sport — work where the result is physical and authority
is earned by doing rather than conferred. Malefics in the tenth are read as recognition
that arrives slowly and does not reverse; Mars is the clearest instance of it.

## Where its aspects land

From the tenth, Mars aspects the first, fourth and fifth. The aspect on the fourth is
the one to name: it falls on domestic peace, which is exactly the fourth–tenth tension
the sources are specific about. That is a tension to raise in a reading, not a verdict
to deliver.

## The caution

The risk is authority exercised by force, not authority withheld. The defensible reading
concerns how the native works — no statement about rank, income or success belongs here.
