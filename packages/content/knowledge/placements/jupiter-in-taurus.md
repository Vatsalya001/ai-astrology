## The dignity, and an asymmetry worth knowing

Venus rules Taurus, and Jupiter counts Venus an enemy — shatru kshetra. The enmity runs
one way only: Venus's own table of friendships counts Jupiter neutral. Classical
friendship is not symmetric, and this is one of the clearer places to notice it.

An enemy sign is a mark against strength and nothing like debilitation. The two get
collapsed constantly and should not be.

## Fixed earth on a planet of increase

Taurus does not redirect. Jupiter placed there is traditionally read as expansion that
proceeds by duration rather than by reach — one thing enlarged over a long period
instead of several things begun. The strength and the limitation are the same property.

## Accumulation rather than income

Jupiter signifies wealth as distinct from earnings, and fixed earth is the most literal
terrain that signification has. The traditional reading is holdings that grow by being
kept. No placement indicates an amount, a gain or any financial outcome, and this is
the one most often read as though it did.

## The Moon's exaltation complicates the verdict

Taurus exalts the Moon, whom Jupiter counts a friend. The sign is unfriendly to Jupiter
through its ruler and hospitable through its strongest dignity. Where the Moon actually
sits with Jupiter here, the configuration has its own name and its own document in this
corpus.

## The caution

Jupiter enlarges whatever it is given, and what Taurus gives it to enlarge is holding
on. Attachment to what has been built is the traditional note, and it applies to
positions and opinions as much as to possessions.
