The Moon in the second house places mind in the house of what is held — resources
already acquired, speech, food, the household the native came from. Of those, the
combination classical texts develop furthest is the Moon and the voice.

## Where the exaltation argument holds, and where it does not

Taurus is the Moon's exaltation sign, and Taurus is the natural second sign. That
coincidence is where this placement acquires a reputation for strength it does not
always earn. Dignity belongs to the sign, not to the house number: the second house is
Taurus for an Aries ascendant and nothing else. For a Libra ascendant the second house
is Scorpio, and the identical house position is a debilitated Moon. Both are correctly
described as the Moon in the second, and they do not read alike.

## Speech, and what the native feeds on

Second-house planets set the register of speech. The Moon is traditionally read as
speech that carries feeling — persuasive by warmth rather than by argument, and
inclined to say whatever the mood supplies. Sources pair this with attachment to food
and to the comfort of a familiar table, treating both as one appetite described in two
places.

Family here means the household of origin rather than the one the native makes. The
Moon's presence is read as emotional identity still bound up in that household.

## A caution

The second house invites financial statements, and a changeable planet in it invites
more. Nothing in this placement supports a claim about how much a native will have or
keep. The defensible reading is that resources are held with feeling attached — which
shapes decisions, not totals.
