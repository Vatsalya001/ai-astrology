The Moon in the fourth house is the most internally consistent placement in this set,
and the one classical texts hedge least. The significator of emotional nature occupies
the house of emotional foundation, and nearly everything else follows from that.

## Directional strength

The Moon holds its dig bala in the fourth — the house where it is directionally
strongest, a distinction it shares with Venus. This is a separate axis from sign
dignity and it holds whichever sign occupies the house. A fourth-house Moon in a
hostile sign carries directional strength and sign weakness at once, which is a real
condition rather than a contradiction to be argued away.

Cancer is also the natural fourth sign, so for an Aries ascendant this is the Moon in
its own sign with directional strength on top. That is the strongest version of the
placement, and it belongs to one ascendant rather than to the house.

## The disagreement worth knowing

Some classical sources apply karako bhavo nashto — the significator seated in its own
house damages the matter — and the fourth-house Moon is the cleanest candidate for it,
since the Moon signifies the mother and the fourth house is the house of the mother.
Other sources ignore the maxim outright, and those that use it apply it chiefly to
Jupiter in the fifth.

A reading should know the dispute exists rather than quietly pick a side. The honest
position is that the maxim is thinly supported and inconsistently applied, while a
strong fourth-house Moon reads well on every other measure available.

## A caution

This is the placement most likely to touch something the native has not raised.
Material about the mother and about early home life belongs in tentative framing with
room to be wrong, and a strong indication here is not licence to tell a native what
their childhood was.
