The ninth is the most auspicious house in the classical scheme, the one texts call
bhagya bhava — fortune in the sense of what is given rather than arranged. Ketu's moksha
signification meets a dharma house here, and the result is the placement classical
sources endorse most readily after the twelfth.

## Mula, and a pattern worth seeing once

Ketu rules Mula, the opening 13°20' of Sagittarius and so of the natural ninth sign. Its
deity is Nirriti and its business is roots — what sits underneath a thing. Set beside
Ketu's other two nakshatras, Ashwini in Aries and Magha in Leo, a pattern emerges: Ketu
holds the first third of each fire sign, which is to say the opening of each natural
trine house. The classical texts do not build this argument, but the arrangement is real
and it is the clearest structural reason to read Ketu more favourably in the first,
fifth and ninth than its general reputation implies.

## Inherited belief, held loosely

The reading most consistent across sources is a native who does not carry the family's
faith, philosophy or law forward — not in rebellion, which would be a Rahu reading, but
through absence of grip. Doctrine studied thoroughly and believed lightly. Where the
ninth is otherwise strong this is read as genuine inquiry; where it is weak, as drift.

## The father, tentatively

The ninth holds the father in most schemes, and Ketu is read as distance rather than
rupture — often geographic, often a relationship that simply did not demand much. This
deserves the same careful framing as fourth-house material about the mother: a reading
is probably touching something the native has not raised.

## What this does not license

A favourable placement is not an outcome. Nothing here supports a claim about travel,
study abroad, a legal matter, or fortune arriving.
