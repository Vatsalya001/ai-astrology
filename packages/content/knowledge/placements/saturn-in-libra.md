## The dignity

Exalted (uccha), the highest of the six grades, with the exaltation degree traditionally
at 20° Libra. Venus rules Libra and is Saturn's friend, so maximum dignity and a friendly
dispositor coincide. This is the strongest Saturn can be by sign, and it outranks both of
its own signs — exaltation sits above swakshetra in the classical order, which is the
detail most often got backwards.

## Exaltation is magnitude, not comfort

The grade describes how completely a planet expresses its own nature, and Saturn's nature
is constraint. An exalted Saturn is therefore read as constraint that is thorough,
consistent and difficult to argue with. Libra's contribution is that it operates as
proportion rather than as force. A reading that translates "exalted" into "easy" has
quietly substituted a different word.

## What it is read as doing

Significations of judgment, arbitration, contract and institutional fairness take on
Saturn's duration: obligations entered slowly and then honoured, agreements treated as
weighty. The Sun's debilitation in the same sign is the instructive mirror — the sign
that most strengthens constraint is the one that most weakens unaccommodated identity.

## Sasa yoga, and its real scale

Saturn in Libra in a kendra forms Sasa yoga. Because Saturn spends about two and a half
years crossing the sign, the yoga belongs to everyone born in that window with a
qualifying ascendant, which is a great many people. Report it, and report it without
ceremony.

## Functional note

For a Libra ascendant Saturn rules the fourth and fifth, making it a functional benefic
there. An exalted Saturn in the first house of such a chart is strong on two independent
axes at once — a configuration worth stating explicitly, since the word "malefic" would
otherwise mislead the whole reading.
