The third is an upachaya house, and this is the first of four placements where the
ordinary reading of Rahu inverts. Appetite lands in a house that signifies effort
against resistance, and the classical sources are consistently favourable about it.

## What the texts emphasise

Courage pursued rather than possessed; output at volume; competence in the mechanics of
communication — writing, argument, self-promotion, the hands. Rahu in the third is
traditionally read as a native who continues past the point at which the resistance was
supposed to stop them, and as reach built by repetition rather than conferred by
position.

## Where the sources are quiet

The classical commentary on this combination is thin, and the thinness is informative
rather than a gap in transmission: the third attracts little discussion because this
placement generates few complaints to discuss. Favourable placements are
under-documented in a literature organised around remediation.

## The sibling signification

The third governs younger siblings and peers. Rahu's foreignness attaching here is read
as a peer circle assembled outside the family's, and as sibling relationships that turn
out consequential in an unexpected direction. Family material deserves tentative
framing and room to be wrong.

## The asymmetry worth checking

A strong third says nothing about whether the output compounds — that is the tenth's
business. The third-house document names the pattern: considerable self-starting energy
applied without a structure that accumulates it. With Rahu here the volume is higher and
the question is unchanged.

## The caution

Reach is not the same as landing. The failure mode traditionally associated with this
placement is mistaking the quantity of communication for its effect, which is easy to
name from outside and nearly invisible from within.
