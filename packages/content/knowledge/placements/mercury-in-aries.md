## Dignity: neutral, and that is the whole of it

Aries is Mars's sign, and Mercury's own row in the natural-friendship table counts Mars
neutral. So Mercury here is in a neutral sign — not exalted, not debilitated, with no
friendship to lean on and no enmity to argue against. Classical texts pass over this
combination quickly, and a reading should resist the urge to compensate.

One asymmetry is worth naming because it is where sources part company. Mars counts
Mercury an *enemy* even though Mercury counts Mars neutral, and schemes that compound
both directions downgrade Mercury in Mars's signs accordingly. Which convention is in
use should be stated rather than assumed.

## Movable fire on the faculty of distinction

What Mercury contributes is speech, calculation and the drawing of distinctions. Aries
is movable and fiery, so the placement is traditionally read as those faculties firing
before they have finished: the sentence arrives at speed, the conclusion precedes the
working, and a correction is received as an opposition rather than as information. It
reads well wherever being first is the mechanism and badly wherever being thorough is.

## The solar constraint, and what it implies here

Because Mercury is never far from the Sun, Mercury in Aries requires the Sun in Pisces,
Aries or Taurus. Aries is the Sun's exaltation, so a close Sun-Mercury pairing at full
solar dignity is one of the more frequent forms this placement takes. Read that as a
mind in service of a confident identity rather than as a mind under pressure — the
tradition is deliberately unalarmed about Mercury near the Sun.

## Where its weight actually comes from

Nothing in the classical material singles this placement out. Its significance in any
particular chart comes from the house it occupies, from what shares that house, and
from Mars's own condition as dispositor.
