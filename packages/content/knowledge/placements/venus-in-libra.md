## Own sign and moolatrikona — the degree decides

Libra is Venus's other own sign and, unlike Taurus, it carries the moolatrikona
portion. The first fifteen degrees are usually given as moolatrikona — ranked above
plain own-sign and below exaltation — with the remainder swakshetra. Sources vary a
little on where the boundary sits. The practical consequence is that two charts both
described as "Venus in Libra" can hold different dignities, and only the degree
separates them.

## A friend exalted and an enemy fallen, in one sign

Saturn is exalted in Libra and the Sun is debilitated in it. Venus counts Saturn a
friend and the Sun an enemy, so Venus's moolatrikona is simultaneously the sign where
one of its friends is strongest and where one of its enemies is weakest. Taurus
inverts this exactly, hosting an enemy's exaltation instead. The two own signs are not
interchangeable, and this is the cleanest demonstration of why.

## Movable air

Libra contributes weighing and proportion, with a movable modality that acts on what it
has weighed. Venus in the sign whose concern is the relation between things rather than
the things themselves is read as relational capacity in its most characteristic form:
negotiation, a sense of fairness, an eye for what is out of proportion.

## Why this says less than it appears to

Which is also the difficulty. Planet and sign signify nearly the same thing here, so
the combination is close to a tautology and carries little diagnostic weight by itself.
The informative content lies elsewhere — the exact degree, the house, what aspects it,
and whether the kendra condition for Malavya is met. A reading that stops at the
dignity has said almost nothing, however strong that dignity sounds.
