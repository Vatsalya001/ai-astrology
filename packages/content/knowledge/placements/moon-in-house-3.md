The Moon in the third house sets a receptive planet in the house of the native's own
initiative — siblings, hands and arms, short journeys, and the willingness to act
without asking.

## The upachaya rule does not transfer cleanly

The third is an upachaya house, where classical texts read malefics as strengthened:
the house signifies effort against resistance, so a planet supplying resistance
supplies the conditions for effort. The Moon is the awkward case. A waxing Moon is a
natural benefic and gains nothing from that rule. A waning Moon is classed as weak or
malefic by most sources, and arguably does gain from it. The texts never settle this,
and a reading that invokes "malefics do well in the third" for a Moon has borrowed an
argument written about Mars and Saturn.

## Siblings and a mind in motion

The distinctive note is restlessness. The third governs short journeys and the Moon
governs the mind, so the combination is traditionally read as a mind that moves rather
than settles: frequent small changes of scene, talk used as the mechanism for
processing feeling, and closeness to siblings that can tip into reliance on them.

Writing and the mechanics of communication live here too. A third-house Moon is read as
expression fluent in feeling and less dependable in structure.

## A caution

Restlessness is the easiest thing in this placement to report as a defect, and
classical sources mostly do not. They read it as mobility, which is an asset in some
lives and a cost in others. The useful question is what the motion is in service of,
not whether it ought to stop.
