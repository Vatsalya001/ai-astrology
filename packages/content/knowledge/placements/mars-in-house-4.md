Of the twelve, this is the placement classical texts treat least kindly. The reasons
given are specific, and two of them do not survive inspection as stated.

## Debilitation, and where it actually applies

The fourth holds Cancer in the natural zodiac, Mars's sign of debilitation — which is
true of the house, not of the placement. That correspondence is literal only for an
Aries ascendant; for the other eleven the fourth house holds some other sign, and Mars's
dignity must be read from that sign. The fearsome reputation of "Mars in the fourth"
borrows a debility that most charts carrying the placement do not have.

## Why the fourth is on the Mangal Dosha list

Mars's seventh aspect from the fourth house falls on the seventh in full strength — the
partnership house. This is one classical rationale for the fourth appearing among the
Mangal Dosha positions, and it is sounder than the bare list. Its other two aspects from
here land on the tenth and eleventh, both upachaya, where the same force is read as
productive. The placement is not uniformly difficult; it is difficult in one direction
and useful in two.

## Land, the unambiguous reading

The fourth governs immovable property and Mars governs land, and this is the one
straightforwardly favourable note the sources offer: property acquired and held through
effort, often through dispute. Boundary and inheritance friction is a traditional
association, not a prediction that litigation occurs or how it ends.

## The household's temperature

Classical sources read a malefic in bandhu bhava as a home with heat in it — argument as
the household's ordinary register rather than its failure state. Material about the
mother and about early domestic life needs tentative framing and room to be wrong,
because a reading here is likely to be touching something the native has not raised. It
describes a temperature. It is not a verdict on anyone in the family.
