The Moon in the seventh house sets receptivity directly opposite the self, in the house
of whatever the self meets as an equal and cannot absorb. The seventh is a kendra, so
the placement is angular and consequential regardless of what else is true of it.

## Receptivity facing an equal

The classical reading is emotional orientation toward partnership — a native whose mood
is substantially set by the state of their closest relationship. Sources read warmth,
accommodation, and a preference for company over solitude, alongside a tendency to
adopt the partner's emotional weather as if it were the native's own.

Business partnership and contracts are the seventh's other territory, and the same
mechanism applies there: agreements entered with feeling attached, and difficulty
holding a position against someone who is present in the room.

## Two readings of one placement

The seventh is where the classical literature's gendered asymmetry is most visible —
Venus consulted for men, Jupiter for women — and a seventh-house Moon inherits some of
it. Sources written for male natives read a nurturing spouse; sources written for
female natives read emotional dependence. That difference belongs to the period the
texts come from rather than to the chart, and it deserves comment rather than
repetition.

## A caution

The placement occurs in roughly one chart in twelve, and seventh-house afflictions in
general are common enough that classical texts list extensive cancellations for most of
them. Frequency is the first thing to establish before weighting this at all.

Nothing here supports a statement about whether a native marries, stays married, or
should. What a reading can say is that emotional attention is located in partnership —
a tension to work with, never a verdict to deliver.
