## Neutral by Mercury's reckoning, hostile by Jupiter's

Sagittarius is Jupiter's, and Mercury's row counts Jupiter neutral — so this is a
neutral sign with no exaltation or debilitation of the classical seven anywhere in it.
Jupiter's row, however, names only two enemies in the entire table, and Mercury is one
of them. The dispositor regards the occupant as an opponent while the occupant regards
the dispositor as nobody in particular, and that lopsidedness is the most informative
thing about the placement.

The tradition's reasoning is legible. Jupiter is the general principle; Mercury is the
particular distinction. Each is an obstacle to the other's method, and Sagittarius is
Jupiter's ground.

## Dual fire: detail in service of doctrine

Mercury here is traditionally read as information organised around a conclusion the
native already holds. Strong at the general claim, fluent in moral and legal framing,
persuasive at scale, and impatient with the exception that does not fit. Classical
associations are teaching, law, religious and philosophical argument, long-form
exposition — anywhere the point is the shape of the whole rather than the accuracy of
each part.

## The Gemini axis

This placement sits exactly opposite Mercury's own sign, and the contrast is the
clearest way to read it. Gemini Mercury distinguishes and declines to conclude;
Sagittarius Mercury concludes and declines to distinguish. Neither is the better mind,
and a chart with Mercury in one of them is traditionally examined for whatever supplies
what the placement does not.

## The caution

Reasoning recruited after the fact. The specific warning attached here is a position
arrived at by conviction and defended with argument, which is harder to detect than
simple error because the argument is often sound. No remedy is required for it; noticing
it is the whole of the traditional advice.
