The Moon in the first house puts the significator of mind into the house of the body,
and the classical reading follows directly: temperament becomes constitution. What the
native feels is the thing that registers first.

## The phase does most of the work

No other planet's first-house reading turns so completely on a single variable. A
waxing Moon rising is traditionally read as an open, easy presence; a waning one as a
presence that costs something to maintain. That is the same house position read in
opposite directions, decided by elongation from the Sun rather than by sign. Checking
the phase before saying anything about a first-house Moon is not a refinement of the
reading — it is the reading.

## What the texts emphasise

Classical sources are unusually consistent here: a soft manner, a face that registers
feeling before speech does, acute sensitivity to atmosphere, and a body that answers to
emotional state. They also note permeability — the native takes on the mood of a room
rather than holding out against it.

The first house is the only one that is both a kendra and a trine, so the placement is
structurally well supported whatever the phase turns out to be.

## A caution

The specific risk is that a mood gets read back as a character. The Moon is the
fastest-moving body in the chart, and its house describes where emotional attention
sits, not what sort of person the native is. A first-house Moon is traditionally read
as emotion that is legible from outside — a different claim from emotional instability,
and the second claim is the one natives have usually been handed already.
