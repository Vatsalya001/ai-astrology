Mercury in the first house holds directional strength — dig bala, which it shares with
Jupiter. Of the twelve positions this is the only one where Mercury's strength comes
from geometry rather than from sign, and it holds for every ascendant.

## What directional strength adds

Dig bala is a separate axis from dignity and the two frequently disagree. A Mercury
here in a sign it dislikes is still directionally strong, and the classical position is
that this is one real combined condition rather than a contradiction to be resolved.
Directional strength is read as a planet operating where it is most exposed — which for
Mercury means intellect that strangers register first.

## Where the two significations meet

The first house is read as the manner in which the native meets the world before
deciding how to. Mercury is the faculty of making distinctions. Together these are
traditionally read as a presence constituted by speech: a native taken at the measure of
what they say rather than of how they look or what they have done. Classical texts
attach a quick constitution and a temperament that processes experience by describing it.

## A frequency note worth checking first

Mercury never strays more than about 28 degrees from the Sun, so a Mercury in the first
puts the Sun in the twelfth, first or second and nowhere else. These are read together:
the identity house occupied by intellect, with the Sun behind it, with it, or just past
it. The with-it case is Budha-Aditya, common enough that it carries no distinguishing
weight.

## The caution for this house

The first describes constitution, not worth. Mercury here is read as a mind on display,
and the cost is specific — the native's thinking gets assessed as a feature of their
person. That is a thing to manage. It is not a statement about the quality of the
thinking.
