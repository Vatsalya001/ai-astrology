The Moon in Sagittarius is neutral by dignity, with an asymmetry running opposite to the
Mercury signs: the host is friendly toward the guest, while the guest is neutral toward
the host.

## Neutral, and well received

Jupiter counts the Moon among its friends; the Moon counts Jupiter neutral. Nothing
exalts or debilitates in Sagittarius, so no dignity term weighs against this. The
placement is read as comfortable without being strong, and classical sources are mildly
favourable and brief about it.

## The dispositor carries the weight

With no dignity of its own to work from, the Moon here is read largely through
Jupiter's condition — its sign, its house, whether it stands in its own ground. This is
the practical reason a reading of the Moon in Sagittarius that never mentions Jupiter is
incomplete. It is also a different situation from Virgo, where an exalted dispositor
turned out to lend the Moon nothing: here the dispositor's goodwill is part of the
reading rather than a fact standing beside it.

## Dual fire and the need for meaning

Sagittarius aims at a principle. Applied to the significator of mind, the combination is
traditionally read as emotional security contingent on the situation making sense —
consolation arriving as an explanation rather than as comfort. The dual modality keeps
it from fixing on one answer: the conviction can be exchanged for a better one without
the temperament underneath it changing at all.

## Belief used in place of comfort

That substitution is the traditional caution here. The note is that a difficulty promptly
reframed as a lesson is a difficulty not yet felt, and that the generosity this
placement is read as carrying is markedly easier to extend than to accept.
