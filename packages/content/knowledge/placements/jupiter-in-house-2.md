The second is the only house belonging to none of the four classifications — not a
kendra, not a trine, not a dusthana, not an upachaya. Jupiter here is read through the
house's own significations with no positional modifier to argue about, which is
unusual enough in this system to be worth saying first.

## Where the texts are confident

This is one of the few placements the classical sources describe without hedging:
accumulated resources, good food, and eloquence. They reach for abundance language here
more readily than almost anywhere else. The second's natural sign is Taurus, ruled by
Venus, whom Jupiter counts an enemy — a reason to read that enthusiasm as being about
the house rather than about any dignity.

## Speech

Jupiter in the second is traditionally read in the register the house already governs:
the teacher's voice. Fluent at length, inclined to explain, trusted partly on how it
sounds. The matching reservation is volume — an answer larger than the question, and
counsel continuing past the point it was wanted.

## The aspect carries the cost

From the second, Jupiter aspects the sixth, eighth and tenth. Two of those are
dusthanas, and expansion on the sixth and eighth is not a gift. This is the one Jupiter
placement whose aspect set is harder to read than its position; the tenth is the
compensating third.

## The caution

A supported second describes capacity to retain, not an amount. It is not a statement
that resources arrive or stay — the eleventh governs income, and a financial reading
needs both houses rather than either.

The classical maraka rules that attach to this house are deliberately not developed in
this corpus, and Jupiter's presence here does not change that.
