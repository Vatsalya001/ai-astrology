Rahu owns nothing and is exalted nowhere in the classical scheme, so its standing in
Cancer rests on two plain facts: the Moon rules the sign, and the Moon is traditionally
Rahu's enemy. This is the least comfortable dispositor relationship available to the
node, and unlike the modern exaltation debates it is not contested.

## The eclipse image

Rahu is the ascending node — the geometric point at which a lunar eclipse becomes
possible. Placing it in the Moon's own sign is therefore the configuration classical
imagery is most vivid about, and the vividness is why it attracts bad writing. A node
and a luminary sharing a sign is not an eclipse. Eclipse requires conjunction within
orb, and that is a separate calculation on actual longitudes.

## Movable water

Cancer is receptivity with a boundary, and Jupiter's exaltation sign. Rahu here is
traditionally read as appetite directed at belonging — home, lineage, the inner circle,
being held — and simultaneously as a pull away from the inherited version of those
things. Texts associate the placement with foreign residence and with a household
assembled rather than received.

## The boundary

Popular astrology turns Rahu in Cancer into claims about a native's mind, mother and
emotional health. This corpus does not. Nothing in a sign placement supports a statement
about anyone's mental or physical condition, and the restlessness the texts describe is a
reading of appetite, not a diagnosis.

The Moon's actual position and phase govern how much of any of this applies, and they
vary completely across the eighteen months of a cohort that shares the sign.
