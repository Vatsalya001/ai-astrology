## The dignity question has no classical answer

The exaltation and debilitation table covers seven planets, and Ketu is not among them.
No classical source gives it rulership of Aries or of anywhere else. So the accurate
statement about dignity is that there is none to state: not exalted, not debilitated,
not own sign, not a friend's sign and not an enemy's. What stands in for dignity is the
dispositor — Mars, lord of Aries — whose sign, house and affliction are what a reading
of this placement actually works with.

## Mars at both ends

Several sources remark that Ketu acts in the manner of Mars. Where that convention is
in use, Aries is one of only two signs — Scorpio is the other — in which the planet
Ketu is said to resemble is also the planet disposing it. That does not make the
placement stronger. It makes it more consistent: no second temperament pulls against
the first.

## Movable fire, with nothing wanted from it

Aries ignites. Ketu is traditionally read as capacity unaccompanied by appetite. Taken
together the combination is read as readiness to act paired with little interest in what
the action returns — useful where speed settles the matter, costly where the thing begun
needs someone who minds whether it finishes.

## Ashwini, the one ownership a node has

Ketu rules Ashwini, 0° to 13°20' of Aries: roughly the first forty-four percent of the
sign, so this is ordinary rather than rare. Nakshatra lordship is the only kind of
ownership the nodes hold in the classical scheme, and sources that weight it read Ketu
in Ashwini as operating in territory it governs. That is as close to own-sign dignity as
a node comes, and it is not the same thing.

## The other end

Rahu is necessarily in Libra. Reading the Aries end alone describes half a pattern.
