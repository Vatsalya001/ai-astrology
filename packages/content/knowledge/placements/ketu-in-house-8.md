This is among the most over-read combinations in the whole scheme, and the reason is
arithmetic rather than astrology: the eighth is ayur bhava, the house classical texts
associate with lifespan, and Ketu is the planet those same texts associate with loss.
Put the two together and the popular reading writes itself. It is also where this corpus
is most firmly silent.

## What is not available here

A reading must never produce a statement about death — its timing, its manner, or its
likelihood. The classical longevity methods exist, disagree with one another, were built
for a context in which the question was asked differently, and stay unfalsifiable until
they are catastrophic. Ketu in the eighth changes none of that, and no amount of
traditional framing makes such a statement responsible.

## What the eighth actually offers

Transformation rather than ending: the house is where things change form. Ketu here is
traditionally read as sustained attention to what is not visible — research, forensic
work, the occult disciplines, anything that rewards interest in a subject without
needing a result from it. Classical sources are warmer on this placement than its
reputation suggests, and the depth reading is the defensible one.

## A rulership claim to handle carefully

Scorpio is the natural eighth sign, and some modern schools assign Ketu co-rulership of
it; at least one scheme goes further and exalts Ketu there. Classical texts give Ketu no
sign and no exaltation whatsoever. Treating a modern assignment as dignity quietly turns
this into an own-sign placement it has no classical warrant for.

## A caution on inheritance

The resources of others sit here, and Ketu is read as indifference to a claim rather
than loss of one. No reading may state what a native will or will not receive.
