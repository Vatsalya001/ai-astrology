The seventh is a kendra, and it is also where Jupiter has the least directional strength
available anywhere in the chart. Both are true simultaneously, which makes this the
clearest illustration in the system of two strength measures disagreeing.

## No dig bala, full kendra

Jupiter's directional strength sits in the first; the seventh is opposite it and
supplies none. Yet the seventh is a house of action, and Jupiter in Pisces,
Sagittarius or Cancer here forms Hamsa yoga. A chart can therefore hold Jupiter
sign-strong, yoga-forming and directionally weakest at once. That is a real condition to
report, not a contradiction to resolve. The seventh's natural sign is Libra, ruled by
Venus, whom Jupiter counts an enemy.

## A rule that does not apply here

The classical reservation about benefics and kendras — kendradhipati dosha — concerns a
benefic that *rules* kendras, not one that occupies a kendra. It is regularly
misapplied to occupancy. Jupiter in the seventh is not weakened by that rule.

## What it is read as

Partnership with an instructive quality: a partner read as teacher or advisor, older in
function if not in years, and a native inclined toward the same role in return. In
trade and contracts the same expansion shows as terms that are generous and large.
Classical texts make Jupiter the significator of the husband in a woman's chart and give
this placement extended treatment on that basis — an asymmetry that belongs to the
literature's period and deserves comment rather than repetition.

## The caution

Nothing here indicates whether or when a marriage occurs, and a reading must not say.
The traditional reservation is narrower and more useful: treating a partner as a
student. Expansion in the house of the equal is read as improvement offered where
parity was expected.
