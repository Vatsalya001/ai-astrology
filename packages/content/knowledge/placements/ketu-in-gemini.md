## A dispositor that has to be read before it can be used

Ketu owns no sign, has no exaltation and no debilitation in the classical tables, so the
placement is read through the lord of Gemini. That lord is Mercury, and Mercury is the
one planet whose character is taken from its companions rather than held on its own.
The consequence is specific to this sign: reading Ketu in Gemini is a two-step
inference, because the dispositor must first be read through its conjunctions before it
can say anything about the node. Of the twelve, this is the most indirect.

## The exaltation claim belongs to one scheme only

A modern convention places Ketu's exaltation in Sagittarius and its fall in Gemini.
Another puts the same pair in Scorpio and Taurus. Neither appears in the classical
dignity table, which does not list the nodes at all. The honest handling is to name the
convention; inheriting "Ketu is weak in Gemini" without naming it passes off a modern
choice as a received fact.

## The sign of saying, and a significator with nothing to say

Gemini's business is articulation and distinction. Ketu's significations run the other
way: knowing without acquiring, skill the native does not account for. The combination
is traditionally read as competence that resists being explained — fluency about
everything except the thing actually done well. Dual signs are read as the register in
which transitions happen, which makes this placement's expression less fixed than the
same node in a fixed sign.

## Ardra

Mid-Gemini, 6°40' to 20°, is Ardra — Rahu's own asterism. Ketu there sits in the
territory its counterpart governs while Rahu sits opposite in Sagittarius. Classical
sources do not build an argument from this, so it belongs in a reading as something
noticed rather than something concluded.
