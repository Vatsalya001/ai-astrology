## Mula, and the exaltation that is a convention

Ketu rules Mula, the first 13°20' of Sagittarius — the asterism named for the root. A
Ketu in the opening stretch of this sign therefore stands in territory it governs, which
is the strongest form of ownership available to a point that rules no sign. That is also
the reason one modern scheme calls Sagittarius Ketu's exaltation. The classical table
awards the nodes no exaltation anywhere, and a competing scheme places the same claim in
Scorpio, so the right form of words is to name the scheme.

## Disposed by Jupiter

Jupiter rules Sagittarius and is the dispositor. Sagittarius is Jupiter's sign of
principle and conclusion, and Ketu's own significations run toward release and
liberation, so the two are read as pointing the same way: the significator of letting go
is lodged in the sign concerned with what the effort was for. This agreement is why
several sources treat Sagittarius as the most comfortable of the twelve for Ketu's
nature. Jupiter's own house and condition still decide how much that is worth.

## A target that can move

The sign's dual modality gives this placement something the other fire signs do not
offer a node: the aim can change without the nature changing. Traditionally read as
convictions held lightly and revised without crisis — which reads as flexibility of
belief in some charts and as never settling on one in others.

## Comfortable is not the same as fortunate

The agreement between planet and sign makes the reading coherent, not favourable. Ketu's
indifference landing in the sign of meaning can present as philosophical ease or as
finding the question already answered and declining to ask it. Worldly significations
are not improved by either. Rahu sits opposite in Gemini, where the appetite is for
information.
