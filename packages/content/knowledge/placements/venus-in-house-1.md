The first house is both a kendra and a trine — the only house that is both — so a
benefic standing in it is structurally reinforced twice. For Venus that reinforcement
lands on the most literal house in the chart.

## The most literal of the twelve

The first house is read for the body and for the manner in which the native meets the
world. Venus is the planet of attractiveness, and the classical descriptions of Venus
in the first are unusually physical: grace of form, a pleasant voice, ease of presence.
Of the twelve houses this is where the sources are least abstract — and where their
descriptions have aged least well.

## Sign and birth time

Venus's sign is largely a property of the time of year, for reasons set out in its own
document. Its house is not. The house follows the ascendant, and therefore birth time
to the minute, so Venus in the first is a reading that rectification can overturn where
a sign placement cannot. That is worth establishing before anything is built on it.

Where the first house holds Taurus, Libra or Pisces, Venus stands in its own sign or
exaltation in a kendra, which is the condition for Malavya yoga.

## Occupying is not ruling

Venus occupying the first is a different statement from Venus ruling it, which is the
case for Taurus and Libra ascendants. Occupants are read for visible traits; the
ascendant lord is read for the direction of the whole chart. A chart can carry both,
and the two statements should be kept apart rather than merged into one flattering
sentence.

## The caution

The classical material here is an appraisal of appearance, and a reading must not
deliver one. The durable content is presence and manner — how the native is met — not
a verdict on how they look.
