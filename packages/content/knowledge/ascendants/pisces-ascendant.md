## Jupiter as chart lord

Pisces rising makes Jupiter the chart lord, ruling the 1st and the 10th — body and
occupation under one planet, as Mercury is for Virgo rising. Jupiter's condition is
assessed first, and its house carries double weight because both rulerships depend on
it.

## Mars and the Moon become the benefics

- **Mars** — the 9th (Scorpio) and the 2nd: a natural malefic as the chart's strongest
  trine lord after Jupiter itself.
- **Moon** — the 5th (Cancer), a trine, and the other functional benefic.
- **Jupiter** — 1st and 10th, benefic as lagna lord.

## Mercury's two angles

Mercury rules the 4th (Gemini) and the 7th (Virgo): two angles, no trine — the
kendradhipati condition. Mercury is also debilitated in Pisces and a classical enemy of
Jupiter, so three considerations point the same way, which is unusual.

**Venus** rules the 3rd and the 8th: no trine, one dusthana. **Saturn** rules the 11th
and 12th — the same. **The Sun** rules the 6th alone, the strict functional-malefic case.

Four planets ruling no trine can make a Pisces chart look bleak on paper. That is
structural, not particular: because no planet rules two signs four apart, **every**
ascendant has exactly three trine lords and four planets ruling none.

## Constitution, temperament, and birth-time sensitivity

Traditionally read as a soft, fuller build with notable eyes; the feet and the lymphatic
system are the sign's region. Temperament: impressionable, imaginative, permeable to the
mood of a room — a perceptive instrument and a cost at once.

Pisces is the fastest-rising sign at Indian latitudes and the least common ascendant, at
around 6% of births. It holds the horizon about an hour and a half at Delhi's latitude
against well over two hours for Cancer, so a half-hour uncertainty in birth time is
proportionally more likely to move the lagna out of Pisces altogether.

## A caution

"Functional malefic" means a planet rules a house that asks for work. It does not mean
the planet harms the native, and with four of them the distinction has to be held firmly
or the reading slides into a forecast of misfortune — which the tradition does not
support.
