The classical literature names several hundred yogas. Most are minor, many are poorly
specified, and a few are clearly corruptions of each other. This document is about how
to handle that body of material rather than about any one configuration.

## Examples of the minor category

- **Chamara** — the ascendant lord exalted in a kendra with Jupiter aspecting, or
  variously, two benefics in the 7th, 9th or 10th
- **Shankha** — the 5th and 6th lords in mutual kendras with a strong ascendant lord
- **Bheri** — Venus, the 9th lord and the ascendant lord variously configured
- **Pushkala** — the ascendant lord with the Moon's dispositor in a kendra, aspecting
  the ascendant

Each has multiple incompatible definitions across sources, and each carries a
description of fame, wealth or royal favour.

## Why they are listed here rather than individually

Three reasons:

**The definitions do not agree.** Two competent readers using different sources will
disagree about whether a chart has Shankha yoga, which makes the finding unusable.

**The claims are uniform and extravagant.** Nearly all of them promise eminence. A
system in which several hundred configurations each promise eminence is not
discriminating between charts.

**They crowd out the real signal.** A reading that lists seven minor yogas has spent
its attention on the least reliable material in the system, and the native cannot tell
that from the parts that are well founded.

## What to use instead

The structural material: house lords and their dignities, the kendra-trikona
connections, the Pancha Mahapurusha yogas where they genuinely apply, and the dasha
sequence. All of it is better specified and more informative than the minor yoga
inventory.

If a native asks about a specific named yoga, say what the condition is, whether their
chart meets it, and how much weight it deserves — which is usually "less than its
description suggests".
