Kala Sarpa yoga is formed when every one of the seven classical planets lies within the
semicircle from Rahu to Ketu.

## The condition

All seven — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn — on one side of the
Rahu-Ketu axis, with none in the other half. Some sources require no planet to be
conjunct either node; others allow it. The variant where planets run from Ketu to Rahu
is sometimes called Kala Amrita.

## What it is read as

Modern readings describe intensity, obstruction and a life of marked phases — effort
meeting resistance until some point at which it does not. The usual claim is of
dramatic reversal in mid-life.

## Why this one deserves particular scepticism

**It is largely a modern construction.** The configuration is not prominent in the
classical Sanskrit literature in the form now circulated, and the elaborate
predictions attached to it in popular astrology have no clear textual basis.

It is also **not rare**. The nodes divide the zodiac in half and planets cluster, so a
noticeable fraction of charts qualify under at least one of the competing definitions.

And it is the single most common source of astrological alarm sold commercially. A
reading that presents it as a grave finding is participating in that.

## How to use it

Mention it if asked, say plainly that its textual standing is weak and its frequency is
not low, and read the chart on its actual placements. The nodal axis is worth
discussing on its own terms — see Rahu and Ketu — and that discussion does not need
this yoga.
