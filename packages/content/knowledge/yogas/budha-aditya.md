Budha-Aditya yoga is formed when Mercury and the Sun occupy the same house.

## The condition

Mercury conjunct the Sun, in any sign. Most sources require them in the same house
rather than within a specific orb.

## What it is read as

Intellect in service of identity: the classical reading is a native whose thinking and
whose sense of self are aligned, producing clarity of expression and administrative
capability. Texts associate it with scholarship, with government service, and with
work requiring both authority and precision.

## The honest caveat

This yoga is **extremely common**. Mercury never travels more than about 28 degrees
from the Sun, so it shares the Sun's sign for a large fraction of all charts — far
more often than not. A reading that presents it as a distinguishing mark is
misdescribing the sky.

There is also a real tension with combustion. Mercury close enough to the Sun to form
this yoga is often close enough to be combust, and classical sources disagree about
whether the yoga survives that. The reasonable position is that Mercury very close to
the Sun is read as a mind serving the native's identity rather than examining it —
which is the shape of a persuasive person rather than an impartial one.

## How to use it

As a supporting indication for intellectual or administrative capacity, weighted by
Mercury's own dignity and by how close the conjunction is. Never as a conclusion.
