Vipreet Raja yoga is formed when the lord of a dusthana — the 6th, 8th or 12th house —
occupies another dusthana.

## The conditions

Three named variants:

- **Harsha** — the 6th lord in the 6th, 8th or 12th
- **Sarala** — the 8th lord in the 6th, 8th or 12th
- **Vimala** — the 12th lord in the 6th, 8th or 12th

The reasoning is that a lord of difficulty, placed in difficulty, damages the house it
would otherwise afflict. Two negatives, read as a positive.

## What it is read as

Resilience and advantage arising from circumstances that should have been bad.
Classical texts associate it with natives who prosper through adversity, through
others' misfortune, or in fields that deal with difficulty professionally — medicine,
law, insurance, crisis work.

## The honest caveat

The logic is appealing and the mechanism is not well explained in the sources. There
are three dusthanas and three lords to place, so the configuration arises in a
substantial minority of charts, and the classical claims for it are large.

It is also the yoga most likely to be offered as consolation — "your difficult chart is
actually a hidden strength" — which is a use the material cannot support. A reading
should name it as resilience in a specific domain and leave the grand version out.

## How to use it

Note which variant is present and which house it protects. The useful statement is
about where the native copes well, not about eminence.
