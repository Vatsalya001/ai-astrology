The classical literature contains configurations named for poverty — daridra yoga and
several relatives, generally involving the 11th lord in a dusthana, or the 2nd lord
afflicted and badly placed.

This document exists to say what to do with them, because the answer is not obvious
from the texts.

## What the sources say

Various combinations are described as indicating loss of wealth, debt, or inability to
retain resources. The descriptions are specific and bleak, and they appear in
respectable sources.

## Why a reading does not report them

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**It is not actionable.** A native told their chart indicates poverty has been handed
something they cannot use. There is no remedy proportionate to the claim and no
decision it informs.

**The claim is not supportable.** Financial outcome depends on economy, inheritance,
education, health and circumstance to a degree that no planetary configuration
addresses. The classical texts were written in a context where material position was
far more fixed at birth than it now is, and their confidence does not transfer.

**It causes harm.** A fatalistic financial prediction changes behaviour — it
discourages effort and attracts exploitation by whoever offers to remove it. This is
the mechanism by which astrology does real damage to people, and declining to
participate is the whole of the fix.

## What can be said instead

The structural reading, which is true and useful: whether the chart indicates
resources consolidate readily or with effort, where earning capacity is indicated, and
what the native's relationship to retention looks like. See the second and eleventh
houses, and the dhana yoga document.

This is not softening a hard truth. It is declining to assert something the material
does not establish.
