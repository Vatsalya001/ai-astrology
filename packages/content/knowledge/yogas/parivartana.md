Parivartana yoga is formed when two house lords occupy each other's houses.

## The condition

The lord of house A sits in house B, and the lord of house B sits in house A. A mutual
exchange — not merely one lord displaced.

Classical sources divide the exchanges into three classes:

- **Maha Parivartana** — between two auspicious houses, read as strongly favourable
- **Khala Parivartana** — involving the 3rd house, read as mixed
- **Dainya Parivartana** — involving the 6th, 8th or 12th, read as difficult

## What it is read as

The two houses become linked: their significations are read as conditional on each
other. An exchange between the 2nd and 11th lords ties income to retention; between
the 5th and 9th, merit produced to merit received; between the 1st and 7th, self to
partnership.

This is the mechanism worth understanding — not whether the exchange is "good" but
which two areas of life the chart has fused.

## The honest caveat

Exchanges are reasonably common, and the three-class scheme is cruder than the
configurations it classifies. A Dainya exchange between the 6th and 11th lords —
difficulty and income — is read negatively by the class rule and is a recognisable
pattern of someone who earns through solving problems.

## How to use it

Name the two houses and read their significations as coupled. The class label is a
starting heuristic, not the finding.
