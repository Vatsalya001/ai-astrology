Aspect in the Vedic system is **whole-sign** and **asymmetric**, and both properties
differ from the Western convention. Reading one with the other's rules produces wrong
answers consistently.

## The general rule

Every planet aspects the **7th house from itself** — the sign directly opposite — in
full strength. This is universal and is the only aspect some planets have.

## The special aspects

Three planets have additional full-strength aspects:

| Planet | Aspects, counted from itself |
|---|---|
| **Mars** | 4th, 7th, 8th |
| **Jupiter** | 5th, 7th, 9th |
| **Saturn** | 3rd, 7th, 10th |

The Sun, Moon, Mercury and Venus aspect the 7th only. Rahu and Ketu are given 5th, 7th
and 9th by some schools and no aspect at all by others; sources do not agree, and which
convention is in use should be stated.

## Why it is asymmetric

Because the aspects are counted forward by sign. Saturn in the 1st aspects the 10th;
Saturn in the 10th aspects the 7th, not the 1st. **Aspect is not mutual** unless the
two planets happen to be 7 signs apart.

This is the error most often made by readers trained on Western astrology, where
aspects are angular and therefore symmetric. Here, A aspecting B does not imply B
aspecting A.

## Whole-sign, not orb-based

A planet aspects every degree of the target sign equally. There is no orb, no
partial strength by degree, and no "applying" or "separating" distinction in the
classical whole-sign scheme.

Some schools use graha drishti with degree-based strength (the sixty-fourths of
*Bhava Chalita* and related methods), and that is a legitimate variant. It is a
different system and should not be mixed with whole-sign counting in one reading.

## What an aspect means

The aspecting planet's significations are brought to bear on the aspected house and its
occupants. A benefic aspect is read as support; a malefic aspect as pressure. Neither
is an event — an aspect describes a standing condition in the chart, present from birth
and permanent.

## Why Jupiter's aspect matters most

Jupiter aspects the 5th, 7th and 9th — three houses, and in the trine positions.
Classical texts treat Jupiter's aspect as the single most protective influence
available in a chart, and checking what it falls on is worth doing before concluding
anything about difficulty.
