Two or more planets in the same house are read as combining their significations. This
is the strongest form of planetary interaction in the system and the one most
frequently named as a "yoga".

## How a conjunction is read

Not as an average. The classical approach is to ask what each planet does to the other:

- **The stronger planet dominates.** Dignity, directional strength and whether either
  rules the house all matter
- **Natural friendship** decides whether the combination is productive or frictional.
  See the friendship notes in each planet's document
- **Mercury is the exception** — it takes the character of its companion rather than
  contributing a fixed one, which is why Mercury's conjunctions matter more than its
  own dignity in many readings

## Combustion

A planet within a few degrees of the Sun is read as combust and weakened. Sources give
different orbs — typically 3° to 17° depending on the planet — and this is one place
where degree matters in an otherwise whole-sign system.

Mercury is combust more often than not, given how closely it orbits the Sun, and
classical texts treat it less severely for Mercury on exactly that basis.

## The useful question

A conjunction is informative when the two significations genuinely interact. Moon and
Mars — feeling given force. Mercury and Jupiter — precision with breadth. Venus and
Saturn — attachment with duration.

It is less informative when a named yoga has been attached to a common conjunction and
given an extravagant description. Budha-Aditya yoga is the clearest example: Mercury
and the Sun share a sign in most charts.

## What to check before weighting one

How many degrees apart. Whether either is combust. Whether either rules the house they
sit in. Whether a third planet is involved, which changes the reading substantially.
